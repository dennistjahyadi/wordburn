# Bet on turbo, keep small in reserve

Download **`ggml-large-v3-turbo-q8_0.bin` (874.2 MB)**: one multilingual file that covers Spanish, German, Dutch and Indonesian. It is the only file a phone can plausibly run that brings all four under about 6.5% WER on FLEURS: **es 3.6, de 4.1, nl 5.7, id 6.3**. The next size down, medium, sits at 9.9–10.2% for Dutch and Indonesian at almost the same file size. Speed is the weak point. The most favourable published phone measurement puts turbo q8_0 at **roughly 40–55 s per 60 s of audio on an A54**. That is on the 45 s line before thermal throttling, and it was measured with ARM kernels (KleidiAI, int8 dot-product) that whisper.rn 0.7.4 does not compile. On the stock build turbo will very probably miss the budget. So the commitment has two conditions: a patched whisper.rn arm64 build with `+dotprod`, and a measured run on the A54. The fallback is **`ggml-small-q8_0.bin` (264.5 MB)**, estimated at 10–35 s per minute, comfortably inside the budget. Small is caption-grade for Spanish (5.6%), marginal for German (10.2%), and not good enough for Dutch or Indonesian (16%+ on read speech, **31% on real Indonesian speech**). If turbo does not fit, launch Spanish and German on small and hold Dutch and Indonesian back. Two engine findings change the plan whichever model wins. First, the app computes DTW word timestamps and then throws them away. Second, omitting `language` in this build means English, not auto-detect.

## Only large-v3-turbo gets all four languages under 7% WER

The four languages are not equally hard. At every size Spanish is easiest and Indonesian hardest, with **Dutch almost exactly as hard as Indonesian** at base and small. In the Whisper paper's FLEURS table, base scores 33.0% for Dutch and 33.1% for Indonesian, one word in three wrong. Small halves that to 16.4% and 16.3% ([Whisper paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04356)). The multilingual base q8_0 is the same 81.8 MB as the bundled `base.en` and would fit the APK like-for-like. It is unusable for half the languages, which is why every serious option here is a download.

| Model (ggml file) | Download | es FLEURS | de FLEURS | nl FLEURS | id FLEURS | es / de / nl / id Common Voice |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base q8_0 | 81.8 MB | 9.9 | 17.9 | 33.0 | 33.1 | 19.6 / 24.5 / 29.5 / 36.1 (CV9) |
| small q8_0 · q5_1 | 264.5 · 190.1 MB | 5.6 | 10.2 | 16.4 | 16.3 | 10.3 / 13.0 / 14.2 / 18.4 (CV9) |
| medium q5_0 | 539.2 MB | 3.6 | 6.5 | 9.9 | 10.2 | 6.9 / 8.5 / 8.0 / 11.6 (CV9) |
| **large-v3-turbo q8_0 · q5_0** | **874.2 · 574.0 MB** | **3.62** | **4.05** | **5.69** | **6.31** | no per-language CV figures published |
| large-v3 q5_0 | 1,081 MB | 2.8 | 4.9 | 5.2 | 6.1 | 4.7 / 5.7 / 4.3 / 7.2 (CV15) |

Base, small and medium rows come from the Whisper paper's Tables 11 and 13 ([Whisper paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04356)). The large-v3 row comes from OpenAI's own language-breakdown chart ([openai/whisper](https://github.com/openai/whisper/blob/main/language-breakdown.svg)). The turbo row is a third party's run of about 150 FLEURS sentences per language ([Whisper Notes](https://whispernotes.app/blog/qwen3-asr-vs-whisper)). File sizes are the byte counts on the upstream Hugging Face repo ([ggerganov/whisper.cpp](https://huggingface.co/api/models/ggerganov/whisper.cpp/tree/main)). Every figure strips punctuation and casing before scoring.

Because the turbo and large-v3 rows come from different harnesses, turbo's German "beating" large-v3 is noise. What survives the mismatch is that turbo sits in large-v3's band and well clear of medium on Dutch and Indonesian. OpenAI's own description agrees: turbo "performs similarly to large-v2" across languages, and it degrades most on Thai and Cantonese, not on any of these four ([openai/whisper #2363](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/2363)). That makes **medium the dominated choice**: 539 MB at q5_0 against turbo's 574 MB, and less accurate in every language.

The best model outright is large-v3, and it is the wrong download. It is 1.08 GB even at q5_0, with a 32-layer decoder where turbo has 4. It also carries a documented hallucination problem: whisper.cpp's maintainer told users "there is something wrong with the v3 large model" and to fall back to v2 ([whisper.cpp #1490](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/discussions/1490)). Turbo gives up at most a point of WER for a decoder an eighth the depth. On a CPU-only phone, that is the only "best model" that can actually run.

Benchmarks flatter every size on this app's audio. FLEURS and Common Voice are read sentences, and short-form video is spontaneous speech. The one Indonesian study on varied real speech puts zero-shot whisper-small at **30.87% WER overall and 38–41% on spontaneous speech**, against 16–18% on the read benchmarks ([arXiv 2410.08828](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08828v1)). Nobody has published the same test for turbo. Expect every number in the table to roughly double on a creator's talking-head clip, which makes small's Dutch and Indonesian unshippable and turbo's merely good.

Quantisation is the one axis that costs little. On large-v3, q8_0 and q5_0 score within noise of f16 (5.25% and 5.19% against 5.35% on LibriSpeech), and 4-bit is where degradation starts ([whisper-quantization-wer-degradation](https://github.com/yoarajota/whisper-quantization-wer-degradation)). A phone-targeted turbo fine-tune measured every quant within +0.27 points of f16 on-device ([Aivo](https://huggingface.co/Aivo/whisper-large-v3-turbo-et-verbatim-2604-ggml)). **Nobody has measured ggml quantisation on non-English speech**, so q5 against q8 is an A/B for the app's own clips, not a settled fact.

Two things WER will not show. The first is German capitalisation. Whisper is trained on cased, punctuated text, but it can fall into a "no-punctuation mode" ([openai/whisper #290](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/290)), and one user reported large-model German output entirely in lowercase ([faster-whisper #601](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper/issues/601)). A burned-in German caption with lowercase nouns looks broken to every native reader. The second is the alternatives. Official Distil-Whisper is English-only and points multilingual users at turbo ([distil-whisper](https://github.com/huggingface/distil-whisper)). The one community multilingual distil skips Indonesian and trails turbo by its own card ([multi7](https://huggingface.co/bofenghuang/whisper-large-v3-distil-multi7-v0.2)). The Parakeet support that arrived in whisper.rn 0.7.0 ([whisper.rn releases](https://github.com/mybigday/whisper.rn/releases)) covers European languages only, per the package README, so it cannot be the single file. A German-only turbo fine-tune with a ggml build scores 4.28 against the original's 6.31 on Common Voice 19 ([cstr ggml card](https://huggingface.co/cstr/whisper-large-v3-turbo-german-ggml)). That makes it a credible later per-language upgrade, at the cost of a second 0.9 GB download.

## Turbo's speed depends on kernels whisper.rn does not compile

Turbo cuts large-v3's decoder, not its encoder. It keeps the full 32-layer, 1280-wide encoder, which by architecture costs about 7× small's and about 2× medium's, while its 4-layer decoder costs about what small's does per token. whisper.cpp encodes a fixed 30 s window whatever the call contains; the app's own `src/asr/whisper.ts` says so in the comment on `MAX_CHUNK_MS`. **Turbo's run time is therefore the number of encoder passes times the encoder cost, and almost nothing else.** No dated measurement exists on an Exynos 1380, so the estimate rests on two anchors that disagree by about 3×.

| Candidate | Peak RAM | A54 s per 60 s, optimistic anchor | A54 s per 60 s, pessimistic anchor | Against the 45 s budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **turbo q8_0** | **1.5–2.1 GB measured on two phones** | **~40–55** | ~150–180 | on the line at best; over on the stock build |
| turbo q5_0 | ~1.2–1.8 GB (estimate) | ~45–75 | slower still | over |
| **small q8_0** | **≈0.9 GB (f16 measured); less at q8** | **~8–16** | ~25–40 | inside, with margin |
| medium q5_0 | ≈2.1 GB (f16 measured) | not estimated | ~75–125 | over |
| base q8_0 | ≈0.4 GB | — | ~10–16 | inside; unusable for nl and id |

The optimistic anchor is a 2026 model card. It ran a turbo fine-tune at q8_0 on a Snapdragon 732G phone at **80.4 s per minute**, on whisper.cpp v1.9.4 built with KleidiAI and runtime CPU-variant selection, with flash attention off ([Aivo](https://huggingface.co/Aivo/whisper-large-v3-turbo-et-verbatim-2604-ggml)). The 732G has two big A76 cores; the A54 has four A78 cores at 2.4 GHz. Scaling by 1.5–2× gives 40–55 s. The pessimistic anchor is an RK3588 board with four A76 cores at 2.4 GHz, the closest CPU to the A54's big cluster. There, `-t 4` ran base at 12.7, small at 32.4 and medium at 98.4 s per minute. Quantisation and build were unstated, and peak RSS matched the f16 memory table ([Turing Pi](https://turingpi.com/whisper-cpp-piper-tts-arm64-turing-pi-rk3588/)). Turbo, scaled from medium by the encoder/decoder split, lands at 150–180 s.

**The optimistic anchor does not describe this app's engine.** whisper.rn 0.7.4 vendors whisper.cpp 1.9.3 (`node_modules/whisper.rn/cpp/whisper.cpp`, `whisper_version()`). Its Android CMake builds the best arm64 library with `-march=armv8.2-a+fp16` and nothing else, and ggml's int8 dot-product kernels and ARM repack GEMMs are compile-time gated on `__ARM_FEATURE_DOTPROD` (`node_modules/whisper.rn/android/src/main/CMakeLists.txt`, `cpp/ggml-cpu/arch/arm/quants.c`). The A54's cores have the instructions, but the library never uses them. A stock A54 will therefore land somewhere between the anchors, and 45 s is unlikely. Adding `+dotprod` to that CMake target, with a runtime `asimddp` check in `RNWhisper.java`, belongs in `patches/` next to the DTW patch. Measure turbo only after that patch is in.

Three more factors push against turbo. First, **the app pays more encoder passes than the benchmarks do**. It packs VAD spans into chunks of at most 28 s, one `transcribeData` call each, so a minute of unbroken speech is at least three full 30 s encodes. A long-form benchmark slides its window forward and pays nearer two. Second, sustained load throttles phones: LLM inference studies show 15–40% drops within minutes ([arXiv 2603.23640](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23640v2)). A cold single run understates the third clip in a queue. Third, flash attention is not the lever it is on desktops. The same model card measured 41 s per window with it on against 13.5 s with it off on a phone CPU ([Aivo](https://huggingface.co/Aivo/whisper-large-v3-turbo-et-verbatim-2604-ggml)), so the DTW patch forcing it off costs nothing here.

**q8_0 rather than q5_0**, because speed is the constraint most likely to rule turbo out. The 5-bit formats need bit unpacking that ARM has no fast path for, and older ggml timings on Apple silicon put q5_0 behind q8_0 ([whisper.cpp #838](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/discussions/838)). The phone-oriented model card ships q8_0 as its recommendation ([Aivo](https://huggingface.co/Aivo/whisper-large-v3-turbo-et-verbatim-2604-ggml)). q5_0 saves 300 MB of download and about 300 MB of RAM, so it is the first thing to try if memory rather than time turns out to be the wall. RAM matters because this build **never memory-maps a model**. Every loader streams the weights into heap buffers (`android/src/main/jni.cpp`, and no `mmap` anywhere in `cpp/`), so turbo q8_0 costs its full 874 MB resident plus state, next to a React Native app with Skia and a video decoder. That is plausible on an 8 GB A54 and tight on the 6 GB model.

## Small is the fallback, and it cannot carry Dutch or Indonesian

If turbo misses 45 s even with the dotprod build, the budget points at small q8_0: 264.5 MB, an estimated 8–40 s per minute, and under a gigabyte of RAM. Its accuracy splits the four languages cleanly. Spanish at 5.6% FLEURS and 10.3% Common Voice is caption-grade. German at 10.2% and 13.0% is marginal but shippable behind the existing low-confidence chip, which is how this app already handles words the engine is unsure of. Dutch and Indonesian at 14–18% on read speech, and **31% on real Indonesian** ([arXiv 2410.08828](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08828v1)), would make the transcript editor the product. The honest fallback is to **launch es and de on small and hold nl and id** until turbo fits.

Two other levers sit between the models. Relaxing the budget to about 60–70 s per minute for downloaded languages covers the optimistic estimate after a 10–30% throttling penalty, and it is a product call rather than an engineering one. Reducing encoder passes is the engineering one. A clip's cost is its chunk count, so packing that fills each 28 s chunk, rather than stranding a 4 s tail in a window of its own, is worth a third of turbo's run time on some clips.

## Pin the language, read the DTW, and keep the paper's guards

**Language is fixed per project and never omitted.** The binding documents `'auto'` as its default, but the JSI layer only sets `params.language` for a non-empty string. An omitted language falls through to whisper.cpp's default `"en"` (`node_modules/whisper.rn/cpp/jsi/RNWhisperJSI.cpp`, `src/NativeRNWhisper.ts`). Auto-detection, where it does run, is one extra encoder pass per `whisper_full` call. At one call per 28 s chunk that nearly doubles turbo's cost, and each chunk can pick a different language. Users report language switching as a turbo failure mode ([openai/whisper #2363](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/2363)). Ask for the language when the project is created. If detection is ever offered, detect on the first chunk, read `result.language`, and pin it for the rest of the run. Keep `translate: false`, four threads (the big cluster only; threads spilling onto little cores made a Pixel 4a slower, [whisper.cpp #1248](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/issues/1248)), and flash attention off.

**The DTW preset becomes per model: `large-v3-turbo` for the download, `small` for the fallback, `base.en` for the bundled English model.** The patch already maps all three strings (`node_modules/whisper.rn/cpp/jsi/RNWhisperJSI.h`), and turbo's six heads on decoder layers 2–3 exactly match OpenAI's own `_ALIGNMENT_HEADS` mask and the Hugging Face generation config ([openai/whisper `__init__.py`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/whisper/main/whisper/__init__.py); [HF generation_config](https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo/raw/main/generation_config.json)). Today `DTW_PRESET` is a constant, `'base.en'`, in `src/asr/models.ts`. Left as it is, it fails turbo's context init, because a head lands on a layer turbo does not have ([whisper.cpp #2480](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/issues/2480)). On small it would pass the range check and silently use the wrong heads.

**The larger point is that the app does not use DTW at all.** `transcribeChunk` builds every word from the heuristic `segment.t0` and `t1` and reads only the per-token probability; nothing in `src/` or `app/` reads `tDtw`. The brief says per-word timing matters. So the multilingual work should also decide whether words come from `tDtw` or from the heuristic path, and measure both. Evidence on turbo's timing quality is thin. A transformers user reported incorrect word timestamps on turbo ([transformers #37248](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/issues/37248)). One project found turbo's DTW word starts within 0.1 s of a reference implementation's for over 90% of words ([mstar PR #318](https://github.com/mstar-project/mstar/pull/318)), which shows parity with that implementation, not accuracy against ground truth. `t_dtw = -1` on repetitive stretches ([whisper.cpp #2329](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/issues/2329)) needs a fallback to `t0`/`t1` for that token.

**Hallucination guards come from the paper and from the v3 family's record.** Keep Silero VAD gating. It targets the phantom lines in silence and music ("thanks for watching", "subtitles by") that v3 users report, alongside repetition loops of 5–45 s ([whisper.cpp #1490](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/discussions/1490)). Turn off previous-text conditioning (whisper.cpp's `-mc 0`, which users credit with "a big difference", same thread). Keep the paper's temperature fallback: step 0.2 up to 1.0 whenever average log-probability falls below −1 or the gzip compression ratio exceeds 2.4, with no-speech above 0.6 treated as silence ([Whisper paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04356)). The paper also uses 5-beam search to suppress looping. Turbo's decoder is small enough that beams are cheap, so A/B beam 5 against greedy rather than assume either. Leave `PROMPT_BIAS` off. A punctuated, correctly cased prompt in the target language is the known fix for German casing ([openai/whisper #290](https://github.com/openai/whisper/discussions/290)), but this app has already watched a badly shaped prompt drop half an English transcript. It earns a per-language A/B before it ships.

## Eight measurements on the A54 decide it

None of the speed or memory numbers above were taken on the A54, and the most important one cannot be taken anywhere else. The pass lines below are this report's proposals; no published caption-acceptability threshold exists.

| Measure | Setup | Proposed pass line |
|---|---|---|
| Processing time per 60 s | turbo q8_0 and q5_0, small q8_0 and q5_1; stock and `+dotprod` builds; 4 threads; release build; the app's own pipeline on real clips; a cold run and the third back-to-back run | turbo ≤ 45 s on the **third** consecutive clip |
| Peak memory | `dumpsys meminfo` during transcription with the editor and player open, on a 6 GB A54 if one exists; whisper.cpp's init log for compute and KV buffer sizes | no low-memory kill of the app; background kills noted |
| WER on the app's own clips | 10 minutes of spontaneous, short-form speech per language, hand-corrected | ≤ 10% for turbo in every language |
| German casing and punctuation | the same German clips scored case-sensitively; count lowercase nouns | no lowercase-mode runs |
| Word timing | `tDtw` against `t0`/`t1`, both against hand-marked onsets, per language | median onset error within one 30 fps frame (33 ms), p95 ≤ 150 ms |
| Hallucination | clips with music beds, silence and room noise | zero phantom lines and zero repeat loops after VAD |
| q5 against q8 | WER per language on the same clips | adopt q5_0 only if within 0.5 points |
| Chunk count | encoder passes per clip under current packing | informs whether packing is worth a slice |

The download also needs a real-world test: 874 MB over mobile data, resumed after interruption, checked by exact byte count the way `scripts/fetch-models.sh` already checks the bundled files, and with the language's captions offered only once the file verifies.

## Sell one setup and no upload, not speed

Competitors claim unmeasured multipliers: Submagic "10x faster", Vizard "10x faster than manual clipping", and OpusClip, which has walked its own figure down to "5x faster" ([submagic.co](https://www.submagic.co/); [vizard.ai](https://vizard.ai/); [opus.pro](https://www.opus.pro/)). They quote clip counts ("30+ clips", about 5 clips per minute of input, [klap.app](https://klap.app/)) and accuracy percentages ("99%", "99.9%"). Every one of those is a cloud claim. Wordburn cannot honestly borrow the multiplier: the proposed non-English budget is 45 s per minute of audio, so wall-clock time is about 0.75× the audio length at worst. It should not borrow an accuracy percentage either, because the best model here is 3.6–6.3% WER on read speech and worse on real speech. Google Play caps the title at 30 characters and the short description at 80, and bans "#1", "best", store-performance words and promotional pricing ([Play Metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en)). It also bans any claim of functionality the app does not deliver ([Play Deceptive Behavior](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888077)). "Free" is doubly risky with a watermarked free tier. "Offline" is now risky too: billing touches the network, and non-English captions need a one-time download first. "On-device" and "no upload" are strictly true.

What is true and uncontested is structural. One setup covers a whole queue, the phone runs it while the user does something else, and nothing is metered, where every auto-clipper meters by minutes, plan or credits ([opus.pro](https://www.opus.pro/); [descript.com/clips](https://www.descript.com/clips)). Batch captioning of many separate clips is also rare on Play, where the category is almost entirely one-long-video auto-clipping ([Kalakar listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.kalakar.app)). That makes it the better headline. **The "20 videos" figure should become a measured throughput claim**, "an hour of clips captioned in about N minutes on a Galaxy A54", filled in only from the A54 runs above. A clip count belongs only where the app enforces it as a cap.

| Use | Copy | Characters |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | Queue the clips. Pick one style. Walk away. | 43 |
| Headline | One setup. A whole queue of captioned clips. | 44 |
| Headline | Your phone captions the queue while you do something else. | 58 |
| Headline | Captions made on your phone. No upload, no minutes to buy. | 58 |
| Headline (only if 10 is the enforced cap) | Drop in a long video. Get up to 10 captioned shorts. | 52 |
| **Play title** | **Wordburn: Captions & Clips** | **26** |
| Play title, alternate | Wordburn: Batch Captions | 24 |
| **Play short description** | **Queue your clips, pick one style, walk away. Captions made on your phone.** | **73** |
| Short description, with clipping | Caption many clips in one style, or cut shorts from one long video. On-device. | 78 |
| Short description, once languages ship | On-device captions in English, Spanish, German, Dutch and Indonesian. | 69 |

Avoid "at once" if the queue runs sequentially, "viral", "in seconds" and any hours-saved figure without a measured baseline. The only published baseline is 5–10 minutes of manual work per minute of video ([3Play Media](https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/long-take-manually-caption-videos/)), a strawman against other auto-caption apps. Neither batch captioning nor auto-clipping exists in the codebase today, where v1 is English-only. Every line in the table goes live only with the feature it describes.

## Conclusion

This decision rests less on which model is most accurate than on how the engine was compiled. Turbo's accuracy case is settled. Its speed case depends on int8 kernels that this whisper.rn build leaves out and that one patched CMake line could restore. The cheapest next step is therefore not a model download but a dotprod build and a single `whisper-bench` run on the A54, which will collapse a 3× spread of estimates into one number. The work also surfaced a gap that exists for English today: the DTW timings the app pays for are discarded. Fixing that improves every language, bundled or downloaded.

The marketing follows the same discipline. The claims competitors lean on, multipliers, clip counts and accuracy percentages, belong to server farms, and on a phone they would be misleading or losing comparisons. The one number worth printing is the one the A54 will produce, and the product owner's instinct to trade "20 videos" for the most reliable figure means measuring before writing a word of it.
