# Lead with no credits; the prices hold

Measured only against caption-first apps, Wordburn's advertisable strengths are mostly absences that its on-device design makes structural, and its prices hold. The three strongest ad claims, in order, are that **nothing in the app can run out** (no credits, minutes or export caps on any plan), that **the free tier is the whole product with no account, no card and no ads**, and that **English captions work the moment the app is installed, with no upload and no signal**; behind them sit the word-level editor, emphasis taken from the voice, and a preview that matches the export. Those rank first because they answer the category's most frequent one-star complaints, which are about credits, billing and stalled server processing, not about caption quality ([Trustpilot: Captions](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/captions.ai?stars=1)). On price, **$39.99 a year is the second-cheapest yearly among caption subscriptions with real distribution, $9.99 a month ties Captions Basic, Zeemo and Kaptioned at the top of the mobile band, and $5.99 a week equals Captions' own weekly** ([App Store: Captions](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/captions-ai-edits-your-video/id1541407007), [App Store: Kaptioned](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/kaptioned-automatic-subtitles/id1518425078)). Pro being four things does not break that, because every comparable at those prices also sells captions and nothing else. The exposed flank is real: nine languages with eight behind the paywall where rivals include 50 to 125, eighteen styles where rivals list 30 to 500, and two small on-device Android apps that offer more on paper for $4.99 or for free with ads ([Google Play: Subly](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=biz.andxor.subly), [Google Play: Offline AI Auto Captions](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=tech.bytesweavers.offlineautocaptions)). No ad should carry a speed claim, an accuracy claim, a count, or the word "only", and two of Pro's four features still have to run on a real phone before they are filmed.

## Seven strengths survive the comparison, and the first three are absences

Two rules shape the ranking. Complaint themes are what an ad should say and praise themes are what it should show: five-star reviews across the category praise ease, accuracy and time saved, so an ad that leads on "accurate" competes with every rival's own reviews, while one that leads on the absence of the one-star causes does not ([Trustpilot: Captions](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/captions.ai), [Trustpilot: Submagic](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/submagic.co)). And every shot should be a screen recording from a real phone, because that recording is itself the hardware check several features still lack.

### Provably true and differentiated, in ad order

| Rank | Strength | One-line hook | Status on real hardware |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nothing to run out of | "No credits. No minutes. Nothing to run out of." | A plan fact; single-clip export has run on a phone |
| 2 | Free is the whole app | "No account. No card. No ads. Every style, every export, one small watermark." | Editor and export run on a phone; film the watermark build on one |
| 3 | Works the moment it is installed | "Turn on airplane mode. Caption the video anyway." | English model inside the app has run on a phone |
| 4 | Word-level editor with sound | "Tap the wrong word, hear it, fix it. The timing does not move." | Run on a phone |
| 5 | Emphasis from the voice | "The word you lean on is the word that gets big." | Run on a phone in the older presets |
| 6 | Preview matches export | "The preview is the file you get." | Checked frame against frame on a phone |
| 7 | Batch of twenty | "Queue twenty clips, pick one style, put the phone down." | Emulator only; hold until it runs on a phone |

**Nothing to run out of** ranks first because credits are the most frequent one-star theme in the category and the most resented mechanism in it. Captions meters by credits, Zeemo by a yearly credit pool, MixCaptions by minute packs, Submagic at 15 to 100 videos a month and Zubtitle at 10 to 30 ([Captions Help: credits](https://captions.ai/help/docs/ai-usage), [Zeemo pricing](https://zeemo.ai/pricing), [Submagic](https://www.submagic.co/pricing), [Zubtitle](https://zubtitle.com/pricing)). The reviews are written in exactly these words: "the generation process never started...my credit was deducted" on Captions, and "I'm supposed to pay a yearly subscription in order to pay more money for time credits?" on MixCaptions ([Trustpilot: Captions](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/captions.ai?stars=1), [JustUseApp: MixCaptions](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/952426779/mixcaptions-video-subtitles/reviews)). Wordburn's claim is structural, since no server does the work and so nothing is metered, and it holds on the free tier too, which beats Bytesweavers' three projects a day ([Google Play: Offline AI Auto Captions](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=tech.bytesweavers.offlineautocaptions)). The limit of the claim: AutoCap, SubCap and Kaptioned publish no caps either, so it differentiates against the credit-metered leaders and the web tools, not against the whole field.

**Free is the whole app** ranks second because it answers three complaint themes at once: ads and intrusive watermarks on Android free tiers, card walls, and trials that hide what the paid product contains. Captions gives its free plan one template and a lifetime pool of credits; Zeemo's free tier is one minute at 720p under a watermark a Play reviewer called "huge and invasive, covering the face of speakers"; AutoCap's carries a watermark, a five-minute cap and ads; SubCap reviewers say it demands a payment card before any auto-caption ([Captions Help](https://captions.ai/help/docs/subscriptions), [Zeemo on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cn.bluepulse.caption), [AutoCap on Aptoide](https://autocap.en.aptoide.com/app), [SubCap on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ratel.subcap)). One Android caption app's reviewer reports watching "atleast 10 ads" for a 30-second short ([Google Play: AI Auto Captions](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=geeks.appz.autocaptions)). Wordburn's free tier, with the full editor, all eighteen styles and unlimited full-resolution exports, is more generous than every free tier that states a count or a resolution cap. It is the combination that is unique: Subly matches the unlimited exports and drops the watermark but runs ads, and Captions has no watermark but meters and locks styles ([Google Play: Subly](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=biz.andxor.subly)). The hook must always carry "one small watermark" and the free-tier pitch belongs in English-language ads only.

**Works the moment it is installed** is the reliability version of on-device, which is how users actually feel the cloud. Nobody in the reviews read complains about privacy; they complain that Submagic's site "is often down for days at a time", that Captions slowed processing to hours after the first day, and that SubCap "insists I have no internet connection when clearly I do" ([Trustpilot: Submagic](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/submagic.co?stars=1), [App Store: Captions reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/captions-ai-edits-your-video/id1541407007?see-all=reviews), [SubCap on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ratel.subcap)). Wordburn's English model ships inside the app, where both on-device Android rivals work only "after an optional one-time model download" or "after one model download" ([Google Play: Offline AI Auto Captions](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=tech.bytesweavers.offlineautocaptions), [Google Play: Subly](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=biz.andxor.subly)). An airplane-mode demonstration is therefore true, filmable and beyond what any rival found can show at first launch. It is an English-only claim: the other eight languages need the 874 MB download first.

**The word-level editor** is the strongest product claim on Android specifically. Captions documents dragging a single word's start and end, but lists that timing control for iOS only; its Android app gets spelling, emphasis, supersize and delete ([Captions: adjust timing](https://captions.ai/help/docs/captions/adjust-timing), [Captions: word effects](https://captions.ai/help/docs/captions/word-effects)). Subly, Bytesweavers and Caption.io all describe retiming a cue, line or segment ([Google Play: Subly](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=biz.andxor.subly), [Google Play: Caption.io](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.prakhar.caption)). The audio half has a ready-made foil in an AutoCap review: "there's no audio during playback so you can't hear the words to edit the captions properly" ([JustUseApp: AutoCap](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1466743701/autocap-video-captions/reviews)). The honest wording is a description of what Wordburn does, not a superlative, because six rivals describe their timing tools too loosely to rule out an equal.

**Emphasis from the voice** has no documented equivalent in any caption-first app. Five rivals advertise automatic emphasis and every one that describes it says "keywords"; one, ClipCap, advertises a "random word highlight" ([Captions styles](https://captions.ai/help/docs/captions/styles), [Submagic blog](https://www.submagic.co/blog/reasons-to-use-submagic-for-ai-captions), [Google Play: ClipCap](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=in.dealempire.clipcap)). It ranks fifth and not first for two reasons: no review anywhere asks for it, and nobody has yet tested whether Wordburn's picks match what a listener hears as stress. It is the best purely visual moment available, a speaker leaning on a word and that word growing, so it should be shown and described, never claimed as unique.

**Preview matches export** has more complaint evidence behind it than its low rank suggests, but users rarely name it. Reports of exports that differ from the preview exist for AutoCap ("words were smushed together that hadn't been smushed in the preview"), Submagic, SubCap, MixCaptions and Zeemo, and two iOS apps' own release notes admit fixing such mismatches ([AutoCap on Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.laika.kinetictypovid), [Skybreak review of Submagic](https://skybreakai.com/blog/submagic-review-2026), [App Store: Subtitles AI](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/subtitles-ai-video-audio/id1247835812)). One 500-install Android app makes the same promise in its listing, so the claim is rare, not exclusive ([Google Play: Auto Captions by Teleprompter LLC](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mkapp.captioning)). Wordburn can prove it on camera with a split screen of preview and exported file.

**Batch of twenty** is differentiated and not yet provable. No mobile caption app above 100,000 installs advertises a queue; Bytesweavers' ten is the only published limit, Subly's queue is described for transcription only, and Zeemo's batch upload sits on its $13.39 Expert plan with no stated limit ([Google Play: Offline AI Auto Captions](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=tech.bytesweavers.offlineautocaptions), [Google Play: Subly](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=biz.andxor.subly), [Zeemo pricing](https://zeemo.ai/pricing)). Wordburn's queue does the whole job per clip, in the background, and resumes after the app is killed, which no listing describes. It stays out of ads until it has run on a real phone, and even then with no duration attached.

## Nine languages, eighteen styles and two free on-device rivals are the exposed flank

**Languages are the largest gap and the paywall makes it worse.** Captions lists 100+, Zeemo 95, SubCap 125+, Submagic 123, and on-device Subly and Bytesweavers 100+ and 50; Wordburn has nine ([Zeemo pricing](https://zeemo.ai/pricing), [Submagic languages](https://www.submagic.co/languages-supported), [Google Play: Subly](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=biz.andxor.subly)). No competitor found charges for an individual language; they gate volume, resolution and translation instead. Wordburn's free tier is English only, so for a Spanish or German speaker the "whole product for free" pitch is false and Pro behind a seven-day trial is the product, plus an 874 MB download that the app offers only on phones with enough memory. The rivals' counts are Whisper's full list offered without any quality bar, whereas Wordburn's nine were chosen on error rate and font coverage, but that is an argument buyers will not see on a store page. Translation, which Zeemo and AutoCap sell, is absent altogether.

**Styles lose on count and on a few controls.** Eighteen presets sit above AutoCap's seven and below everything else that publishes a number, and letter spacing and line height are shipped by a 500-install Android rival ([Google Play: Auto Captions by Teleprompter LLC](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mkapp.captioning)). Active-word highlight is table stakes in 2026, and glow and a card behind the block are now named by the newest on-device apps.

**The on-device niche is contested, not vacant.** Subly lists 100+ languages, a personal dictionary, a queue, 4K export and no watermark, free with ads and one $4.99 item; Bytesweavers lists 50 style recipes, 50 languages, batch of ten and speech hints for a one-time purchase ([Google Play: Subly](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=biz.andxor.subly), [Google Play: Offline AI Auto Captions](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=tech.bytesweavers.offlineautocaptions)). Both have 1,000 and 500 installs and no ratings, and none of their claims has been tested, so nothing is known about how they behave on a real phone. They are the two apps to install and run before any ad is cut.

**The rest is execution risk.** Batch and the eight languages, half of what Pro sells, have run only on an emulator, several styles have not run on hardware, and nobody has yet completed a purchase. The app is Android only against cross-platform rivals with ratings from tens of thousands of reviewers. Captions' free plan and Subly both export without a watermark where Wordburn's free export carries one. The capped dictionary is a weak Pro item when the nearest rival's appears free and uncapped. And the demand evidence has a hole: no review read asks for batch, offline use or no-signup, Reddit was unreachable, and the frequency ranking rests on roughly sixty quoted or summarised reviews, so the ranking above is directional, not measured.

## 5.99 / 9.99 / 39.99 holds with Pro at four things

**Yes, all three prices hold against caption apps only, and the yearly is the offer.** The decisive point is that the comparables never included anything but captions at these prices: Captions Basic at $9.99 a month sells captions plus a credit pool, Kaptioned at $9.99 sells watermark removal, Zeemo Pro at $9.99 sells credit-metered captions capped at three minutes a video ([Captions Help](https://captions.ai/help/docs/subscriptions), [App Store: Kaptioned](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/kaptioned-automatic-subtitles/id1518425078), [Zeemo pricing](https://zeemo.ai/pricing)). Narrowing Pro to four caption features leaves it level with what that band already buys.

**$39.99 a year is the strongest number.** It is below Captions ($64.99), Zeemo ($79.99), Kaptioned ($49.99), SubCap and Captiono ($47.99), and above only AutoCap ($29.99), and it equals RevenueCat's North American yearly median to the cent, against category medians of $29.99 for Photo & Video and $34.99 for Utilities ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps), [RevenueCat 2026 Business](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-business/)). There is no room above it now: the three caption apps at $47.99–$49.99 include every language they support, so a higher Wordburn yearly would charge more for nine. **$9.99 a month is the weakest number and still holds**, as an anchor. It ties the three largest apps and the Utilities median, but it is double AutoCap and Captions Lite, the category leader's own Android app on the same store. With the yearly preselected that is the monthly's job: in Superwall's cases a default yearly moved yearly selection from 37% to 63% ([Superwall](https://superwall.dev/blog/the-superwall-newsletter-volume-1)). **$5.99 a week** matches Captions' weekly and the cross-category weekly median, sits between Kaptioned and SubCap, and at 7.8 times the yearly over 52 weeks stays an anchor instead of a bargain. The **seven-day trial on yearly** is more than any mobile caption app states in its listing, and five-to-nine-day trials convert at a 37.4% median against 25.5% for four days or fewer ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)).

What each of the four things is worth differs by buyer, and that decides who the ads should target. For an English-speaking single-clip creator, Pro is watermark removal plus an uncapped dictionary; the category prices that at $2.50 to $4.17 a month on yearly plans, so $3.33 is fair and $9.99 monthly is steep. For a volume creator, batch has no priced equal among the large apps: the nearest are Zeemo's batch upload on a $13.39 plan and Bytesweavers' ten-clip queue inside a one-time purchase. For a non-English creator, Pro is the entire product, and $39.99 for unlimited captions still undercuts every large cloud subscription. The price risk comes from below, not from the band: one-time prices exist at $4.99 (Subly), $8.99 (AutoCap) and $3.99–$19.99 (Bytesweavers), and a buyer who compares on Play will see them. They do not justify a cut today, because the cheapest on-device rivals have no ratings and negligible installs, and no evidence shows buyers choosing them. If a number moves first it is the monthly, to $7.99, the SubCap and Captiono price and RevenueCat's Photo & Video median, and only if Play Console shows the preselected yearly taking well under the 63% share the default should produce.

## Conclusion

Restricting the comparison to caption apps changes what Wordburn is selling. Against editors and broader suites the pitch was a bundle; against caption apps the bundle argument disappears and what remains is a billing model. The category's anger is aimed at meters, renewals and servers, and Wordburn's architecture removes all three, which is why the top three ad claims are things the app does not have. That implies the on-device fact returns to the front of the marketing as a mechanism ("nothing to run out of, nothing processing on a server") and not as a feature word, and that the store listing and the ads have to say the same thing.

The second implication is about sequence. The claims that separate Wordburn from the two small on-device rivals, the per-word editor, the voice-driven emphasis, no ads and no model download for English, are all verified on a phone today, while the claims that would separate it from them most sharply, batch of twenty and eight more languages, are the unverified half of Pro. The prices do not need work; the hardware runs do, and installing Subly and Offline AI Auto Captions for an afternoon will say more about the next year of positioning than any further desk research.
