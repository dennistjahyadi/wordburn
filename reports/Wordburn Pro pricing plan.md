# Keep the dollar prices, halve the rupiah, retire the renewing week

**The USD prices are right and the rupiah prices are wrong, and the fix is to type three Indonesian numbers by hand rather than to change the base plans.** Wordburn Pro at **USD 9.99 a month and 39.99 a year** sits exactly where the mobile caption category and the cross-industry medians put a creator utility: Captions Basic and Zeemo both charge $9.99 a month, RevenueCat's Utilities median is $9.99 and its North American yearly median is $39.99, and every clipping tool a working clipper would otherwise pay for starts at $12–$19 a month on an annual plan ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/captions-ai-edits-your-video/id1541407007), [RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps), [Submagic](https://www.submagic.co/pricing)). Against the value delivered the price is cheap: one Fiverr order for a single short clip costs $5, a clipper's own schedule puts captioning at 3–4 minutes a clip, and batch plus auto clip removes most of an hour a day ([Fiverr](https://www.fiverr.com/pravezarshad/add-professional-subtitles-and-caption-on-your-short-video), [Reach.cat](https://reach.cat/blog/clipping-editing-workflow-15-clips-day/)). The problem is the home market. Play's auto-conversion produced **Rp 179.000 a month, 40% above CapCut Pro and 2.6× YouTube Premium**, and a **Rp 90.000 week that costs what Canva charges for a month**, in a country whose subscription medians run at 38–46% of North America's ([Kompas](https://tekno.kompas.com/read/2025/01/02/16020077/harga-spotify-capcut-pro-dkk-sudah-kena-ppn-12-persen-di-google-play-store?page=all), [RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). The final plan is **USD 5.99 / 9.99 / 39.99** in the base currency, **Rp 35.000 / 59.000 / 349.000** set by hand for Indonesia, the weekly rebuilt as a **7-day prepaid pass that does not renew**, the trial moved from **3 to 7 days on yearly only**, **yearly preselected** on the paywall, and **no usage caps on any paid plan**. Each of those numbers has a benchmark behind it; the one that does not is the yearly's US price, which is the first thing to A/B after launch.

## The dollar prices are in band; only the yearly's discount is unusual

Every direct mobile competitor with a store listing prices inside a narrow band, and Wordburn's USD placeholders happen to land in the middle of it. Captions (Mirage) lists Basic at **$5.99 a week, $9.99 a month and $64.99 a year**, with a $4.99 Lite plan sold on Android only; Zeemo's app is $9.99 a month and $79.99 a year; AutoCap is $4.99 and $29.99; SubCap charges $7.99 for a week or a month and $47.99 for a year ([App Store, Captions](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/captions-ai-edits-your-video/id1541407007), [Captions Help](https://captions.ai/help/docs/subscriptions), [App Store, Zeemo](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/zeemo-ai-video-caption-tool/id1577284115), [App Store, AutoCap](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/autocap-auto-video-captions/id1466743701), [App Store, SubCap](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/subcap-captions-ai-subtitles/id1571349902)). The cross-industry medians say the same thing from the other direction: RevenueCat's 2025-data medians are **$5.99 / $7.99 / $29.99 for Photo & Video and $5.99 / $9.99 / $34.99 for Utilities**, with North America at $6.99 / $9.99 / $39.99 ([RevenueCat 2026 Business](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-business/), [RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). Adapty's panel, which skews to aggressive monetisers, runs higher at $7.48 / $12.99 / $38.42 ([Adapty 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/)). A $9.99 monthly is therefore the top of the caption-app band and the exact Utilities median, and $39.99 a year is the North American and Productivity median to the cent.

The one ratio that stands out is the yearly's. Against the named competitors Wordburn's **yearly is 0.33 of twelve months, below the 0.48–0.50 floor** that AutoCap, SubCap, Opus Clip and Klap all use and far below CapCut's 0.75 ([Opus Clip](https://www.opus.pro/pricing), [Klap](https://klap.app/pricing), [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/app-used-millions-nearly-doubles-subscription-price-overnight-11535999)). Against the broad market it is normal: RevenueCat's category medians put yearly at 3.5–4× monthly, which is the same 0.29–0.33 ([RevenueCat 2026 Business](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-business/)). The two reference sets disagree because they are selling different things. The cloud caption tools' yearly carries twelve months of metered credits, so it is priced as twelve months; the broad market treats the yearly as the plan it wants everyone on and prices the monthly as the decoy. Wordburn has no credits to meter, so the broad-market shape is the right one, and the steep discount is a feature of the paywall rather than a leak. What the comparison does say is that there is room above $39.99: SubCap's $47.99 and Captions' $64.99 are the nearest yearly prices, and the CapCut backlash of February 2026, when Pro went from about $77 to $179.99 a year, had users naming "alternatives at $50 annually" as the sane price ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/app-used-millions-nearly-doubles-subscription-price-overnight-11535999)). That makes $49.99 the obvious first price experiment and not the launch price, because price changes are the experiment type with the lowest win rate in Adapty's data, **45.5% on LTV against 58.7% for plan-duration tests**, so the structural changes below should land first ([Adapty key findings 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)).

The weekly is the other plan below its benchmark. At $4.99 it is 0.50 of the monthly where Captions charges 0.60 and the category medians sit at $5.99 ([App Store, Captions](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/captions-ai-edits-your-video/id1541407007), [RevenueCat 2026 Business](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-business/)). Both competitors that sell a week treat it as a high-margin impulse purchase, not a cheap door: Captions' week is 4.8× its yearly over 52 weeks and SubCap's is 8.7×. A week priced under the market's own median is the one plan on the paywall that low-intent users will default to, which is Superwall's named risk of three-plan paywalls, so if a week stays on the screen it should move up to **$5.99**, not down ([Superwall](https://superwall.dev/blog/the-superwall-newsletter-volume-1)).

## A clipper's week of captions is worth more than a year of Pro

Whether $9.99 is worth it depends on who is buying, and the research is unambiguous that the buyer is not the median user of a captions app but the person producing clips daily. Captioning by hand costs **five to ten minutes of work per minute of video** for a practised captioner, and a one-hour video is a full day ([MN.gov](https://mn.gov/mnit-accessibility/captioningessentials/intro_howlong.html)); a clipper's own published schedule budgets **3–4 minutes per clip for captions and hooks** inside a three-hour day of fifteen clips ([Reach.cat](https://reach.cat/blog/clipping-editing-workflow-15-clips-day/)). Buying the work instead costs **$5 per short clip at the Fiverr floor**, $1.99 a minute at Rev, and $12–$30 an hour on Upwork, where an hour of audio is billed as six working hours ([Fiverr](https://www.fiverr.com/pravezarshad/add-professional-subtitles-and-caption-on-your-short-video), [Rev](https://support.rev.com/hc/en-us/articles/18893487380365-Pricing), [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/hire/captions-freelancers/)). A clipper doing twenty clips a week spends roughly five hours a month on the caption step alone; at Upwork's rate that is $75 of labour, and at Fiverr's it is $400 of outsourcing, against a $9.99 subscription. Batch and auto clip attack the two largest steps in that schedule, the 65 minutes of cutting and the 50 minutes of captioning a day, which over a five-day week is 8–10 hours ([Reach.cat](https://reach.cat/blog/clipping-editing-workflow-15-clips-day/)).

What that clipper earns sets the ceiling. Campaigns on Whop and Vyro pay **$1–$4 per 1,000 views** with per-clip caps and agency cuts of 20–50%, elite clippers get $500–$1,500 retainers, and beginners are consistently described at $100–$500 a month ([Moneywise via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/articles/clipping-side-hustle-youve-never-110000379.html), [FORKOFF](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/clipping/how-much-do-clippers-earn-2026)). The tool stack those people already pay for is **$30–$70 a month**: Opus Clip at $15–$29 plus CapCut Pro at $19.99, or Submagic at $12–$39 ([Opus Clip](https://www.opus.pro/pricing), [Submagic](https://www.submagic.co/pricing), [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/app-used-millions-nearly-doubles-subscription-price-overnight-11535999)). A $9.99 plan that bundles captions, batch and auto clip is a "replace one line item" pitch under the cheapest of those; anything above $20 has to replace two, and that is the zone where the market has shown refusal. CapCut's doubling drew "forced to upgrade" threads and a free tier quietly capped at five caption generations per thirty days; Captions' credit-metered $24.99 tiers draw "nickel and diming" and "not worth the subscription" reviews ([reelvideocaptions](https://reelvideocaptions.com/blog/capcut-auto-captions-not-free-anymore.html), [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/capcut-pricing), [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/captions-ai-edits-your-video/id1541407007?see-all=reviews)). The strongest sentiment signal in the whole evidence base is about mechanism rather than level: creators object to credits that run out and caps they were not told about. Wordburn's zero marginal cost makes "no credits, no minutes" free to promise, and it is a claim no cloud competitor at $5–$10 can match.

Zero marginal cost is a margin advantage, though, not a pricing one. Raw cloud transcription now costs **$0.0025–$0.0077 a minute**, so a cloud app serving a heavy user 500 minutes a month spends under $4 on ASR and charges $12–$39; the category's prices are set by anchoring and perceived value, and "we have no server" is not a floor a competitor cannot undercut ([AssemblyAI](https://www.assemblyai.com/pricing), [Submagic](https://www.submagic.co/pricing)). That matters for the gating as much as for the price. The watermark is the category's near-universal free-tier marker and the honest claim about removing it is social rather than algorithmic: Instagram's only official statement, from 2021, concerns Reels recycled from other platforms with their logos on, and no platform has said anything about a third-party editor's corner badge, while the "40–60%" and "up to 20%" reach-penalty figures in circulation name no study ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2021/02/10/instagram-reels-with-tiktok-watermark-less-discoverable/)). Creators pay to remove it because every competitor has trained them to read a mark as "free-tier output". Batch behind Pro matches the one on-device competitor, which gates a batch of ten the same way ([Google Play, Offline AI Auto Captions](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=tech.bytesweavers.offlineautocaptions)). Languages are the odd one: Captions advertises 100+, Zeemo 95, Klap 52 and Opus 20+ on the free or lowest paid tier, so **nobody else gates a language**, and the defensible framing of Wordburn's gate is the 874 MB model download rather than the language itself ([Captions Help](https://captions.ai/help/docs/subscriptions), [Zeemo](https://zeemo.ai/pricing)). In Indonesia that gate has an extra edge, because the home market's own language is behind it; the paywall there should lead with "captions in Indonesian" rather than the watermark, and a review saying "harus bayar untuk Bahasa Indonesia" is the first complaint to expect.

## Indonesia pays half of what Play converted, and the week is the worst offender

Play's conversion is an exchange rate with tax on top, not a pricing decision. The Console converts the base price at the rate on the day it was set, adds tax in tax-inclusive countries, rounds to a local pattern and then **freezes the result until "Update exchange rates" is pressed**; nothing in Google's own help describes a purchasing-power recommendation, and the claim that it suggests PPP prices comes only from pricing-tool vendors ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6334373?hl=en)). The observed Rp 90.000 / 179.000 / 690.000 imply a rate in the low-to-mid 16.000s with 11% PPN folded in, which is what Google says it does: it determines, charges and remits **11% VAT** on Indonesian Play purchases and the sticker is tax-inclusive ([Play Console Help, tax](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138000?hl=en)). Indonesian press report store prices with the tax line visible, so Rp 179.000 is the number a buyer compares against the market, and the market looks like this.

The monthly is **39–41% above CapCut Pro**, the most expensive creator tool in the country, and the weekly is 2.3× Canva's week. The yearly is the only one of the three that is merely high rather than out of band, landing between Zeemo and Canva. Three independent yardsticks agree on where the band actually is. The World Bank's 2025 private-consumption PPP factor is **Rp 5.066 per international dollar**, a price level of about 0.31 of the US, which would put Wordburn at Rp 25.300 / 50.600 / 202.600; the Big Mac index has the rupiah 57% undervalued, a ratio of about 0.43 ([World Bank](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/ID/indicator/PA.NUS.PRVT.PP?format=json&mrv=4), [The Burger Index](https://www.theburgerindex.com/blog/currency-rankings-2025)). What subscription apps actually charge in the region sits between those: RevenueCat's India and South-East Asia medians are **$4.61 weekly, $3.75 monthly and $18.32 yearly**, which is 66%, 38% and 46% of North America ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). Strict PPP would price Wordburn under InShot, which is not where a Pro tier wants to be; the RevenueCat band is what the market bears, and the comparables cluster exactly on it.

The grey market says where the real clearing price is. Reseller sites sell CapCut Pro and Canva Pro accounts at Rp 20.000–50.000 a month against list prices of Rp 95.000–129.000, and "cara bayar Spotify murah" guides are a genre ([freemium.id](https://freemium.id/blog/murah-banget-langganan-capcut-pro-di-sini)). A large population pays Rp 20–50k for a creator tool and a minority tolerates the official Rp 95–129k. Indonesian creators also cannot justify a tool on view payouts: India and Indonesia have the lowest TikTok CPMs in the world at **$0.005–$0.01**, so a Rp 179.000 month is more than many creators' monthly view income, and the buyers who can pay are brand-deal and TikTok Shop sellers in a market of 100 million-plus users spending about 45 hours a month in the app ([Awisee](https://awisee.com/blog/tiktok-cpm/), [DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-sub-section-top-social-platforms)). Payment rails are not the obstacle, since Play Indonesia takes GoPay, DANA, OVO, ShopeePay, DOKU and carrier billing on all five operators; price is ([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/2651410?hl=en&co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DID)).

The arithmetic of a lower price is not a loss. Of a tax-inclusive Indonesian sticker roughly **76.6% reaches the developer** after 11% PPN and the 15% subscription fee that governs every market outside the US, UK, EEA, Australia and Japan, against 85% of a US sticker ([Play Console Help, fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en), [Play Console Help, tax](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138000?hl=en)). A Rp 59.000 month nets about Rp 45.200 and a Rp 349.000 year about Rp 267.000, which is a third of a US subscriber-month and almost exactly the gap RevenueCat already reports: realised LTV per payer after a year is **$14 in India and South-East Asia against $32** in North America, with download-to-paid at 0.7% against 2.8% ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). The lower price does not make the Indonesian subscriber worse than the benchmark; it makes them exist. Arbitrage risk, the usual objection, is small for an Android-only app whose Play account country can change once a year with a local payment method, and Google has blocked regional-price exploitation and offered payment-location restrictions since May 2025 ([Google Play Q2 2025](https://google.play/the-latest/2025-q2/)). The one procedural rule that matters is that a price change for existing subscribers triggers Play's consent flow, so the rupiah prices have to be set before the first Indonesian subscriber rather than after ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6334373?hl=en)).

For the other emerging markets the evidence is thinner and the recommendation is correspondingly narrower. India, the Philippines and Vietnam share RevenueCat's pooled medians with Indonesia, so setting them to the same dollar-equivalent is supported by the same data; the World Bank's 2025 factors for those three (19.84 INR, 20.53 PHP and 7.203 VND per international dollar) are a sanity check but no spot rates were sourced, so the local digits should come from the Console's converter rounded to a local pattern ([World Bank](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/ID;BR;IN;PH/indicator/PA.NUS.PRVT.PP?format=json&mrv=1&per_page=10)). Brazil and Mexico matter because Portuguese and Spanish are Pro languages, but the only comparable found is Submagic's BRL list at R$79 monthly against $19 in USD, one data point with no rate behind it, and Türkiye rests on a single Adapty index saying it prices about 3.5× cheaper than the US ([Submagic](https://www.submagic.co/pricing), [Adapty 2025](https://adapty.io/blog/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2025-in-10-minutes/)). Those three should stay on auto-conversion until Play Console has sixty days of per-country paywall and conversion data, and then be hand-set as a second wave at roughly half the US price if the data shows views without purchases.

## The weekly is an anchor that renews; the trial is in the worst cell measured

The weekly plan is where the two big datasets disagree most, and the disagreement itself is the finding. Adapty's panel says weekly plans are now **55.5% of all subscription revenue**, 51% in Photo & Video and 73.6% in Utilities, and recommends leading with weekly plus trial ([Adapty 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/), [Adapty, weekly vs monthly vs annual](https://adapty.io/blog/weekly-monthly-annual-subscription-plan/)). RevenueCat's larger panel says weekly "rarely exceeds 30% of category revenue except Gaming", that **year-one retention of weekly subscribers is 3.4%** and "rarely exceeds 10% at six months", and calls the weekly "a revenue trap" when it cannibalises longer plans ([RevenueCat 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025), [RevenueCat, weekly subscriptions](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/weekly-subscriptions)). Both agree on the shape: weekly wins on conversion and loses on retention, and its pooled LTV only beats annual on cohorts of new trial starters, which is a paid-acquisition model. Wordburn has no acquisition budget and no analytics, so the half of the weekly model that pays is the half it cannot run, and the half that bites is the half it would get: Photo & Video refund rates run **14.1% in APAC** against 6.4% globally, and the billing complaints caption apps draw on the stores are "kept charging me" and "paid but still gated", never "weekly" as such ([Adapty, Photo & Video](https://adapty.io/blog/photo-video-app-subscription-benchmarks/), [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/captions-ai), [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/captions.ai)). None of the six clipping and caption tools a clipper would compare against (Opus Clip, Submagic, Klap, Vizard, Descript, Captions' web tiers) sells a week at all ([Opus Clip](https://www.opus.pro/pricing), [Klap](https://klap.app/pricing), [Captions Help](https://captions.ai/help/docs/subscriptions)).

The region cuts the other way, and it is the strongest argument for keeping a week on the paywall in some form. Weekly plans are 36.4% of subscribers in India and South-East Asia, the highest share outside gaming regions, and Photo & Video's weekly share is 38.8% ([RevenueCat, weekly subscriptions](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/weekly-subscriptions)). An Indonesian clipper who wants a week should be able to buy one. What they should not be sold is a renewal they did not ask for, and Google Play already has the product that threads this: a **prepaid base plan** of one day to one year that "does not automatically renew", can be topped up, and can be switched to an auto-renewing plan inside the same subscription ([RevenueCat docs, Google prepaid plans](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/subscription-guidance/google-prepaid-plans), [Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/140504?hl=en)). A seven-day prepaid pass is the honest version of what the weekly was for: the clipper pays for the week they want, nothing renews, nobody writes a "charged weekly" review, and the yearly still looks cheap beside it. It costs two things. Prepaid plans cannot carry offers, so no trial sits on it, and prepaid buyers never become the 3–5% who forget to cancel, which is the revenue the weekly model lives on and the revenue a one-person app with a personal Gmail for support should not want. It also costs a `prepaid` case in `pro.ts` and a check that expo-iap 5.6 reports a prepaid purchase distinguishably, which no source answers; if that is not done by launch, the right fallback is two plans, which is the most common configuration at 41–60% of apps, with the pass added when it is ready ([RevenueCat 2026 Business](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-business/)).

The trial decision is the clearest in the whole evidence base. RevenueCat's per-plan table over 17,000 apps from August 2025 to July 2026 puts a **3-day trial on an annual plan at 24% conversion and 18.3% first renewal, the worst cell measured**; a 5–9-day trial on annual converts at 33% with 25.3% first renewal, and 17–32 days reaches 44.6% ([RevenueCat, free trial length](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/free-trial-length)). Wordburn's current design is that worst cell. Seven days is the smallest change with the clearest payoff, and it is the right stopping point for a tool whose value is the export: a fourteen-day trial on an on-device app is two weeks of free, watermark-free backlog clearing, which is the burst problem in a new coat. The trial stays on the yearly alone, because that is the plan it is there to convert and because a seven-day free week answers the clipper who was going to buy one; with the trial as the backlog week, the renewing weekly has no job left. Whoever leaves will leave early regardless: **84% of cancellations on 3-day trials happen on day 0–1** and 64% on 7-day ones, so the longer trial costs little in lost conversions among people who were never going to stay ([RevenueCat, first renewal](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/first-renewal-churn)). The platform caveat is that trial-to-paid in India and South-East Asia is **15.2% against 34.2%** in North America, so the Indonesian trial will convert a fifth as well per start; that is an argument for the pass alongside it, not against the trial ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)).

Preselecting the yearly is the largest paywall lever documented anywhere in this review and it costs nothing. Superwall's cases moved yearly selection from **37% to 63%** by making it the default, with 82% of high-intent users taking the preselected yearly and 54% of low-intent users dropping to monthly; a secondary reading of Adapty's benchmarks gives 69% against 28% and $2.99 against $1.94 revenue per paywall view ([Superwall](https://superwall.dev/blog/the-superwall-newsletter-volume-1), [RocketShip HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/optimize-app-paywall-higher-conversion/)). Showing the yearly as a per-month figure beside Play's own string helps, which the app already does. Two further Play settings follow from the data rather than from taste: nearly a third of Play subscription cancellations are involuntary billing failures, more than double the App Store's 14%, so the grace period should be the maximum on every plan and account hold left at its default of 60 days minus grace ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps), [Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/140504?hl=en)).

Usage caps are where the category's convention should not be copied. Every competitor meters per month (Opus Clip 150–300 minutes, Submagic 15–40 videos, Klap 100–300 clips) with capped rollover, because for them the quota is a cost control ([Opus Clip Help](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/where-did-my-minutes-go), [Submagic](https://www.submagic.co/pricing), [Klap](https://klap.app/pricing)). For Wordburn the quota's only job would be to make a period's price buy a period's work, and the prepaid pass does that without a meter: the person who buys a week and runs ten batches has paid for the week they used. What remains of the burst problem is the trial, and Play limits a trial offer to new customers once per account. A per-period cap would import the one complaint class the category has earned, Captions' "charged twice", and would spend the only pricing claim no cloud competitor can match ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/captions-ai)). So: **no caps on any paid plan**, the batch queue's twenty-clip limit stays as a device constraint and not a quota, and "No credits. No minutes." goes on the paywall. If Play Console later shows heavy batch use inside trials that never convert, the fix is to limit batch and auto clip during the trial only, never to add credits to paid plans. The lifetime unlock stays retired: one in four apps sells one, Photo & Video leads adoption, and the common multiple is 3–5× the yearly, but no source quantifies what it does to annual conversion and the project left "pay once" deliberately ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps), [RevenueCat, lifetime subscriptions](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/lifetime-subscriptions)).

**$9.99 monthly** is Captions Basic, Zeemo and the RevenueCat Utilities median, and is the decoy for the yearly rather than the plan to optimise ([App Store, Captions](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/captions-ai-edits-your-video/id1541407007), [RevenueCat 2026 Business](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-business/)). **$39.99 yearly** is the North American and Productivity median exactly and under the $50 that CapCut's refugees name; it displays as $3.33 a month, and $49.99 is the first Play price experiment to run once the paywall has a few hundred views, because Adapty's high-priced annual Photo & Video apps earn **$52 in twelve-month LTV against $19.50** for low-priced ones and price tests are the kind most likely to lose, so it is tested rather than assumed ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/app-used-millions-nearly-doubles-subscription-price-overnight-11535999), [Adapty, Photo & Video](https://adapty.io/blog/photo-video-app-subscription-benchmarks/), [Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2023/05/price-experiments-in-play-console.html)). **$5.99 for the pass** is Captions' weekly and the category's weekly median, 0.60 of the monthly, and 7.8× the yearly over 52 weeks, which keeps it an anchor rather than a bargain ([RevenueCat 2026 Business](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-business/)).

**Rp 59.000 monthly** is Spotify's and Netflix Mobile's price to the thousand, sits between Zeemo's entry tier and YouTube Premium, and is about $3.3–3.6 at the rate Play used, beside the regional monthly median of $3.75; it nets about Rp 45.000 ([Kompas, Spotify](https://tekno.kompas.com/read/2025/08/05/16210047/daftar-harga-langganan-resmi-spotify-di-indonesia-per-agustus-2025-ada-kenaikan), [RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). **Rp 349.000 yearly** is 0.49 of twelve months, the competitor floor, beside Zeemo's Rp 399.000 and under half of Canva's; it displays as Rp 29.000 a month, is about $20 against the regional yearly median of $18.32, and nets about Rp 267.000, which is the $14–17 year-one LTV per payer the region reports ([App Store Indonesia, Zeemo](https://apps.apple.com/id/app/zeemo-keterangan-subtitle/id1577284115?l=id), [RevenueCat 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)). The shallower annual discount in Indonesia than in the US is deliberate: the region's yearly median is 4.9× its monthly where North America's is 4.0×, so the region buys yearly less and the discount need not be as deep ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). **Rp 35.000 for the pass** is 0.59 of the monthly at Captions' ratio, under Canva's Rp 39.000 week, the only local weekly comparable left after Spotify discontinued its Mini plan, and above the Rp 20–50k grey-market band's floor so it does not read as the cheap way in ([Canva](https://www.canva.com/id_id/belajar/canva-pro-murah/), [Kompas, Spotify](https://tekno.kompas.com/read/2025/08/05/16210047/daftar-harga-langganan-resmi-spotify-di-indonesia-per-agustus-2025-ada-kenaikan)). All three end in the 0.000 or 9.000 patterns Play's own rounding produced, and none approaches the Rp 3.000 floor Google set in 2015 ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2015/11/minimum-purchase-price-for-apps-and-in.html)).

Three things have to be true before launch for any of this to matter. The `yearly-trial` offer that the A54 did not receive on 2026-10-01 has to be active, attached to the yearly base plan and eligible for new customers, since the trial is the yearly's whole conversion lever. The rupiah prices have to be typed into the three base plans before the first Indonesian subscriber, because a later change triggers Play's price-change consent flow. And the paywall's Pro pitch in Indonesia should open on captions in Indonesian, not on the watermark, because that is what Pro is there. For India, the Philippines and Vietnam, set the same dollar-equivalents as Indonesia now on the strength of the pooled medians; leave Brazil, Mexico and Türkiye on auto-conversion for sixty days and hand-set them at about half the US price if Play Console shows paywall views without purchases.

What to watch, all of it in Play Console's subscription reports since the app transmits nothing: yearly trial-to-paid against the 33% benchmark for a 5–9-day annual trial and the 15% regional one; month-one cancellations on the yearly against the roughly 30% RevenueCat reports, since **95% of lapsed annual subscribers never return** ([9to5Mac on RevenueCat 2026](https://9to5mac.com/2026/05/27/new-report-shows-annual-app-subscribers-rarely-return-after-they-cancel/)); the refund rate against 7.7% regional and 14.1% APAC Photo & Video; the share of cancellations that are billing failures, which the 7-day grace exists to catch; yearly's share of purchases, which should exceed 60% with it preselected and is the first sign the default is not working if it does not; and the pass's repeat-purchase rate, because a pass bought three times by one account is a monthly subscriber the paywall failed to convert and the cue for a switch-to-yearly offer, which converts 8–14% when shown after repeated renewals ([Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/weekly-vs-annual-subscription-app)). The one experiment worth running in year one is $39.99 against $49.99 on the yearly in the US and Western Europe, where the price is unchanged, with the Indonesian prices held still until there is a quarter of data.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. The placeholders were never a US pricing problem; they were an Indonesian one created by an exchange rate and a tax gross-up that nobody chose, and the whole gap between "is the current pricing worth it" and "what should it be" is the distance between Rp 179.000 and Rp 59.000. The dollar prices survive contact with every benchmark, the value case is so lopsided for a daily clipper that the price is a rounding error against their labour, and the only real risk to the business model is mechanical: a renewing week with 3% year-one retention selling to a region with the highest refund rates in the category, and a 3-day trial on the plan it is meant to convert.

Two things follow that the pricing numbers alone do not say. In Indonesia, Wordburn's free tier is English-only in a market that speaks Indonesian, which makes the home-market product a hard paywall behind a 7-day trial whether or not anyone intended it, and hard paywalls convert five times better than freemium with nearly identical year-one retention ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)); the language gate nobody else uses is, in the home market, the strongest conversion mechanism the app has, and should be sold as such rather than apologised for. And the on-device architecture's real commercial asset is not the margin but the promise it licenses: "no credits, no minutes" is the exact sentence the category's complaints are written against, every cloud competitor is moving the other way, and it costs Wordburn nothing to keep.
