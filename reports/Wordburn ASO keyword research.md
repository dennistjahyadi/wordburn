# Keep the live title, win the long tail

**Keep the title Wordburn already has live, `Wordburn: Subtitles & Captions` (30/30). Use the short description to own "word by word" and "auto captions for videos". Hand-write the title and short description in all eight other languages, with Portuguese, Spanish and Indonesian first. Do not put keywords in "What's new": no source says Google Play indexes it, and two vendors say it does not.** Play ranks a listing on three fields: the title, then the short description, then the full description, where the first 250–300 characters count most. Policy rules out emoji, "free", "best", "#1", ALL CAPS and repeated keywords in those fields. About ten caption specialists, each with 350k to 5.9M installs, hold every English head term ("auto captions", "captions app", "add subtitles to video"). A new app will not take those in its first months. Below them the tail is open, and in places Wordburn is already in it: it ranks **#15 for "word by word captions"**, where the #1 app has 4k installs and no ratings, and **#9 for "burn subtitles to video"**, where the #1 has 782 installs. The draft in `ASO.md`, `Wordburn: Captions & Clips`, would drop "Subtitles", the second-most-common title word in the category. It would replace it with "Clips", which names auto clip, the feature removed on 2026-10-02. It must not ship. The live listing has the opposite problem: it still sells "Offline, on-device, pay once" and "nine caption styles", which the subscription repositioning ended. Fix it before anything else.

## Release notes carry no ranking weight, and three fields carry nearly all of it

Google does not publish a list of indexed fields. Its discovery guidance names only two: a title that should "reinforce what your app is about", and a description that should put "the most important text… on the top half" and follow "SEO best practices" without "keyword spamming" ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/4448378)). Release notes are not mentioned. ASO vendors fill in the rest, and on the main fields they agree. App Radar's 2026 ranking-factor guide gives the **title "the strongest single keyword weight"**, puts the 80-character short description second, and says the 4,000-character full description is "fully indexed" but that "the first 250 to 300 characters carry the most weight". It lists **"Release notes/What's New"** as not indexed, along with the developer name, the package name and in-app purchase names ([App Radar](https://appradar.com/academy/aso-basics/app-store-ranking-factors)). AppFollow agrees that the title outweighs every other field ([AppFollow](https://appfollow.io/blog/google-play-aso-keywords)).

So the answer on release notes is no. **No source anywhere claims "What's new" is indexed, and no controlled test of it exists.** The "not indexed" view is vendor consensus, not something anyone has demonstrated. Even so, there is nothing on the other side. Google's newer AI surfaces do not read release notes either. Ask Play, the Gemini chat built into listings since December 2025, draws on "the description and key features" and on the app's own website. Guided Search builds its AI listicles from "metadata and user reviews" ([AppTweak, I/O 2026](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/google-i-o-2026-aso-updates); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/05/19/google-play-store-gets-new-ask-play-chatbot-as-gemini-starts-recommending-apps/)). The 500 characters of release notes per language are conversion copy. They help someone decide to update or install, and vendors count conversion as a ranking input. Stuffing them with keywords gains nothing and carries some risk under Play's general spam rule.

Review text, unlike release notes, does reach the index. In a published experiment, RadASO found that **48% of Play reviews over 100 characters (16 of 33) were indexed within 2–3 days**, and that **developer replies were indexed within 5–7 days**. Reviews under five words never were ([RadASO](https://radaso.com/asomythbusters-experiments/do-reviews-get-indexed-in-the-app-store-and-google-play)). This is one vendor experiment, and the year is not stated. The policy-safe way to use it is to reply naturally, with "batch captions", "subtitles" or ".srt" when a user wrote those words. Vendors disagree on whether the developer name is indexed: AppTweak says yes, App Radar says no ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/app-store-optimization-aso-checklist-for-google-play)). Either way, putting "Captions" into "Venturefold Labs" is the kind of change the 2021 policy targets.

**The policy constraints are specific.** Google's metadata policy caps the title at 30 characters. For the title, icon and developer name it bans "emojis, emoticons, or repeated special characters", ALL CAPS unless part of the brand, and any text about "store performance or ranking, price or promotional information". For descriptions it bans "repetitive or unrelated keywords", unattributed testimonials, "best"/"#1" claims, and promotional pricing such as "free for limited time" ([Metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842)). The 2021 change that set these rules named the banned words: "top", "best", "#1", "free", "no ads", "new", "discount", "sale", and calls to action such as "download now" ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/how-to-prepare-for-new-google-metadata-policy-changes)). The impersonation policy separately bans titles or descriptions that "mislead users about your app's relationship to someone else or another app" ([Impersonation policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888374?hl=en)).

That last rule reaches less far than `ASO.md` assumes. Naming TikTok, Reels or Shorts descriptively is not forbidden. **27 of 39 caption listings name TikTok in the full description**, and the risk sits in the title and icon, where an implied affiliation is most likely to be read. On density, vendors have mostly dropped percentage targets. App Radar now recommends "2 to 3 natural mentions of your primary keyword" and warns that Google penalises stuffing ([App Radar](https://appradar.com/academy/aso-basics/app-store-ranking-factors)). AppTweak still quotes 2.5–3% ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/app-store-optimization-aso-checklist-for-google-play)).

Ranking is not only text. Google says it weighs "ratings, reviews, downloads" and "the overall experience… based on user behavior" ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/4448378)). It also makes an app "less discoverable" when the user-perceived crash rate exceeds **1.09%** or the ANR rate exceeds **0.47%**, with an **8% per-device** threshold that can hide the app on one phone model ([Android vitals](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9844486?hl=en)). These are the only ranking numbers Google publishes. Two parts of Wordburn put pressure on them: the 874 MB model load, which killed apps on a 2 GB emulator, and the batch queue's long foreground service. Metadata cannot make up for vitals that cross those lines.

Two newer tools matter less than they appear. Custom store listings can now target search keywords, up to 50 listings. But they draw on keywords "known to bring you traffic", which a near-zero-install app does not yet have, and they swap what is displayed rather than earn a ranking ([Custom store listings](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867158?hl=en)). Store listing experiments can test descriptions in localized experiments. **The app name is not testable** ([Store listing experiments](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6227309?hl=en)), so `ASO.md`'s plan to "run it as a store listing experiment" cannot work for the title.

## Ten caption specialists own every head term, and the tail below them is open

Play publishes no search volume. The demand evidence here is therefore ordinal: Play's own autocomplete, which appears after a few letters, and Google Trends, which measures web and YouTube search rather than Play. The two agree on the core. Typing **"capt" suggests "captions" and "captions app"** after four letters. **"subt" suggests "subtitles for videos"**. "add sub" and "add cap" complete to "add subtitles to videos" and "add captions to videos". In US web search, **"auto captions" roughly tripled in interest between the first and last six months of 2024–2026 (20.9 → 56.1)**, and "captions app" did much the same (17.7 → 49.4) ([Trends US](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?date=2024-10-01%202026-09-30&geo=US&q=auto%20captions,add%20captions%20to%20video,captions%20app,capcut%20captions,auto%20subtitles)). Feature words carry almost no demand of their own. In the same window, "word by word captions" scored 0.4 and "animated captions" 0.5 against "auto captions" at 20.4 ([Trends US](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?date=2024-10-01%202026-09-30&geo=US&q=auto%20captions,subtitle%20maker,video%20captions,animated%20captions,word%20by%20word%20captions)).

Competition is the reverse of demand. Two apps rank in the top 5 for **31 of the 46 queries** pulled: Pixster's "Captions AI: Videos Subtitles" (2.76M installs, 92k ratings) and Captions' own Android app, "Captions Lite" (4.47M). SubCap, AutoCap, Zeemo, Dubs, Captiono and quso's "AI Captions" fill most of the remaining slots ([Play search: auto captions](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=auto%20captions&c=apps&hl=en&gl=US)). Submagic has no Android app at all, and the "SubMagic" on Play belongs to an unrelated developer. The cracks show in the bottom half of the top 10. A **1.6-star clone with 72k installs, released April 2026, holds #3–#4 for "captions app"**. An app with 137 installs is #5 for "caption maker" ([Play search: caption maker](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=caption%20maker&c=apps&hl=en&gl=US)). Play is ranking on exact-match title relevance there, not on reputation. A well-titled newcomer can reach slots 4–10, but not the top 3 of a head term.

Two measurements decide how the words are spelled. Play conflates singular and plural closely for the main pairs: "auto caption"/"auto captions" and "subtitle"/"subtitles" each share 9 of 10 results. It treats **"captions" and "subtitles" as different keywords, sharing only 3 of 10 results**, so both must sit in the most heavily weighted field. That supports the live title. It contradicts the `ASO.md` draft. AppTweak's study of 100 singular/plural pairs found the same pattern, with more overlap on Play than on iOS ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/do-singular-or-plural-keywords-rank-differently-in-aso)). Use plurals for the core nouns. Singulars come in naturally through compounds such as "caption styles" and "subtitle editor".

Expectations should match the clock. A new app is first findable by its exact name. Long-tail rankings arrive in one to three weeks, and broad terms in two to six weeks given steady installs and clean vitals. All of these timelines come from vendors or anecdote, and Google publishes none ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/vmzavas/google-play-search-ranking-for-a-new-app-how-it-works-5h2c); [AppFollow](https://appfollow.io/blog/google-play-aso-keywords)). Indexing lag after an edit runs from days to a week. So the fastest readable cadence is **one field changed every one to two weeks**. Changing the title and short description together hides which change moved the numbers.

## Six neighbouring intents share the same words and would cost installs

The creator intent Wordburn serves occupies a narrow band: captions or subtitles *for videos*, auto captions, adding subtitles to a video, and caption styling. Around it sit large neighbouring markets that use the same nouns. They are traps, because ranking for them brings installs that uninstall and leave bad reviews.

**Bare "subtitle"/"subtitles" means movie subtitles.** Downloaders with 1.4–1.6M installs hold #1–#4, and "subtitles for movies" returns nothing but downloaders and players ([Play search: subtitle](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=subtitle&c=apps&hl=en&gl=US)). The word belongs in the title only beside "Captions", with "for videos" carrying the intent in the copy. **Bare "caption" means text for an Instagram post.** Its autocomplete runs to "captions for photos", "ai caption for photo" and "caption for instagram", and its regional completions ("caption apps bangla") point at languages Wordburn does not support. **"Closed captions" and "live caption"** belong to accessibility apps for calls and conversation: Google Live Transcribe, CaptionCall, InnoCaption ([Play search: live caption](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=live%20caption&c=apps&hl=en&gl=US)). **"Karaoke subtitles"** is entirely singing apps such as StarMaker and Smule. **"Word by word" alone** autocompletes to four Quran-study apps. **"Text on video"** belongs to general editors with a median of 144M installs. "Karaoke" and "word by word" are only safe attached to "captions".

The second set of traps consists of terms Wordburn cannot honestly use. **"Translate"** appears in 18 of 39 competitor descriptions, and "subtitle translator" is a Play suggestion. Wordburn does not translate, and its listing already says so. **"Emoji"** and **"100+ languages"** are category staples (Captions Lite claims 100+, Captune 99+), and both would be false. **"No watermark"** appears in 10 listings and in user queries in every market: "subtítulos automáticos gratis sin marca de agua", "subtitle otomatis gratis tanpa watermark". It is false for Wordburn's free tier, which exports **three videos** with a watermark. That clash also predicts a conversion leak: users searching with that qualifier will land, see the mark, and leave. **"Offline", "pay once" and "forever"** came out in the subscription repositioning and stay out. "Offline" is also contested by four small recent entrants (Subly, SubLocal, Identiq's Auto Captions and Bytesweavers' Offline AI Auto Captions), so less is lost than it seems. **"Clips"** and any auto-clip phrasing are gone with the feature. **Brand and person names** ("captions ai", "autocap", "capcut", "hormozi") are navigational searches for someone else. In a title they invite an impersonation complaint. Above all, never pair "Captions" with "AI" in a way that echoes the Captions AI look-alikes crowding the category.

"Transcribe video" is the one adjacent term worth a sentence. It has more Trends interest than "auto captions" (48.9 vs 37.4 in the same US batch) and a weak Play top 10 (median 50k installs) ([Trends US](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?date=2024-10-01%202026-09-30&geo=US&q=auto%20captions,transcribe%20video,srt%20generator,subtitle%20editor,captions%20for%20reels)). But those searchers want text, not burned-in captions, so it earns one natural mention of the .srt export, not a target.

## The English listing should spend its short description on "word by word"

**Title: keep `Wordburn: Subtitles & Captions` (30/30).** It is live, it covers the two non-interchangeable head nouns, and it already ranks for long-tail phrases. The alternatives each lose something. `Wordburn: Captions & Subtitles` (30) is the same keywords in a different order, and nothing published says order matters. `Wordburn: Auto Captions` (23) picks up "auto" but drops "subtitles". `Wordburn: Captions & Clips` (26) names a removed feature. "AI" sits in 23 of 39 competitor titles and is close to meaningless as a differentiator. Leaving it out is a defensible trade, and since titles cannot be A/B tested, it is a trade to make once. The "Word-" game-coding risk that `ASO.md` flags is real. "Subtitles & Captions" next to the brand is what resolves it in a search row.

**Short description: three measured options, none using "free" or a platform name.**

| Option | Text | Chars | What it buys |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A (recommended)** | `Auto captions for videos, word by word. 18 caption styles. Burn in or save .srt.` | 80 | The best head term, the most winnable long tail, "caption styles", "burn" and ".srt" for the file-tool searchers |
| B (volume positioning) | `Auto captions for videos, word by word. 18 caption styles, batch up to 20 clips.` | 80 | Keeps the Pro positioning in the second-weighted field, at the cost of "burn" and ".srt" |
| C (current draft) | `Auto captions for every clip, word by word. One video or a batch of 20 at once.` | 79 | The `ASO.md`/`PLAY-CONSOLE.md` text. Loses "videos", "styles" and ".srt" |

Option A is the evidence-led choice. "Batch captions" shows no demand signal anywhere. Batch has also never run on a phone (CLAUDE.md, Known issues), and a headline claim that misbehaves on an A54 is a ratings risk. B suits the clipper repositioning, if that is the bet. If B ships, the batch claim must stay tied to Pro in the full description.

**Full description: front-load the opening.** The first 250–300 characters do double duty, as the heaviest-weighted description text and as what Ask Play and Guided Search summarise. A suggested opening (257 characters):

> Wordburn adds auto captions and subtitles to your videos, word by word. Pick one of 18 animated caption styles, tap any word to fix it, then burn the captions into the video or export an .srt subtitle file. Caption one clip, or a batch of up to 20 with Pro.

The body of the current `PLAY-CONSOLE.md` §2 text is close to right. Work the remaining tail terms in once or twice each, in the sections where they are true:

- "add subtitles to videos" and "captions for talking videos" in the first section;
- "karaoke captions" and "highlighted word" under the styles;
- "subtitle editor" under editing, where per-word nudge, split and merge set Wordburn apart from the "edit every word" claims Kaptioned and Inverse AI make;
- "personal dictionary", which only Subly also claims;
- "caption maker";
- "batch captions" and "caption multiple videos" in the Pro section;
- the nine languages, named.

Keep "auto captions" to two or three mentions. "Caption your first three videos free… exported with a small watermark" is factual tier text in the description, not promo pricing, and it belongs there and nowhere higher. One descriptive line such as "made for vertical video on TikTok, Reels and Shorts" is within policy and is category practice. But almost every rival has it, so it adds relevance, not rank. Whether it is worth any risk is a call for Dennis, not something the evidence settles.

**Release notes: write for the person deciding to update, and match `free-tier.ts`.** The current draft in `PLAY-CONSOLE.md` §8 says "Single clips still caption and export with a small watermark". That describes the pre-slice-19 tier, in which single clips were unlimited. Since slice 19 the free tier is three videos. A corrected version (392/500):

> Wordburn Pro is here.
>
> Batch captions: queue up to 20 clips, pick one language and one style, and let your phone work through them in the background.
>
> Captions in nine languages: English, Dutch, French, German, Indonesian, Italian, Polish, Portuguese and Spanish.
>
> Your first three videos are free to caption and export with a small watermark. Pro captions as many as you like and removes it.

Localize release notes along with the listings. They are conversion text that local users read. They are not a ranking field.

## Eight hand-written listings, with Portuguese, Spanish and Indonesian first

Play shows a user the listing in their device language if one exists. Otherwise it shows the default listing, with an opt-in machine translation ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9844778)). Google says professional translation "can lead to better search results" ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/4448378)). Its free Console machine translation is **unreviewed and does not cover Dutch, Polish or European Portuguese at all** ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9844778)). The competitors show what machine translation does to this vocabulary. AutoCap's listings read "Subtítulos y subtítulos" and "Legendas e legendas", because "captions and subtitles" collapses into one word. Others pick the photo-caption sense: Spanish "leyendas", Italian "didascalie", Polish "podpisy", Dutch "bijschriften" ([AutoCap es-ES](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.laika.kinetictypovid&hl=es&gl=ES)). The title and short description, about 110 characters per locale, should be written by hand.

Every language has a clear native head term, and in each one the "automatic" form beats the bare noun, which is polluted by other intents. In the less-contested markets, an **exact-match native title takes #1**. "Sottotitoli automatici AI" leads it-IT. "automatische ondertiteling" leads nl-NL, where only four of the top twelve results are caption apps and the rest are unrelated games. "Legenda.ai: Legenda automática" leads all four Portuguese queries tested ([Play search it-IT](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=sottotitoli+automatici&c=apps&hl=it&gl=IT); [nl-NL](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=automatische+ondertiteling&c=apps&hl=nl&gl=NL); [pt-BR](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=legenda+autom%C3%A1tica&c=apps&hl=pt&gl=BR)). The big brands mostly leave titles in English. Captions Lite and AutoCap localize only the short description. Zeemo and SubCap leave Dutch and Polish titles in English. That makes **Dutch and Polish the cheapest title keywords to own**. In English, "captions" is typed in every market, but mostly as the brand "Captions AI" or as "what does captions mean".

Two of these lean on something only Wordburn can claim truthfully: the spoken language itself. Autocomplete shows people asking for exactly that, with "subtítulos automáticos en español" and "automatyczne napisy do filmów po polsku". A competitor claiming 100 languages cannot say "en español" with any more force than Wordburn can. Indonesian gets a half-English title because "subtitle" is the native word there, and the localized short description is the real gain. Those three also produce the richest autocomplete. Portuguese (BR), Spanish (es-419 plus es-ES) and Indonesian come first, then German, French and Italian, then Polish, Dutch and European Portuguese. Dutch YouTube autocomplete returned almost nothing for app-intent seeds, which suggests low volume. Its listing is still worth writing, because "automatische ondertiteling" has almost no competition. pt-PT can be a light edit of pt-BR, but it has to exist: pt-PT devices otherwise fall back to the default listing. Verify the Console locale codes before pasting. Indonesian has historically been `in`, and Spanish needs `es-419` and `es-ES` added separately to serve both.

One honesty point the localized listings must handle: **the app's interface is English-only.** There is no localization library in `package.json` and no translated strings in `src`. A German listing implies a German app. A single line in each localized full description protects ratings in exactly the markets these listings are meant to open, along the lines of "the app is in English; captions are in the language you speak".

## The live listing, `ASO.md` and `PLAY-CONSOLE.md` disagree in five places

The store and the repository have drifted apart.

**The live listing** for [`com.wordburn.app`](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.wordburn.app&hl=en&gl=US) went up on 22 September 2026 under developer Venturefold Labs, with 1+ installs. Its title is `Wordburn: Subtitles & Captions`. Its short description reads "Auto captions & subtitles for your video. Offline, on-device, pay once." Its full description still sells "NINE CAPTION STYLES" and a one-time purchase. Three of those claims are now false: "pay once", the style count, and, by implication, the absence of a subscription. Nobody can buy the old one-time unlock any more, and the product for sale is a subscription. That is the misleading-claims exposure `PLAY-CONSOLE.md` §1 was written to avoid. The listing should be replaced along with the release that ships Pro.

**`ASO.md`** has fallen behind in four ways:

- It proposes `Wordburn: Captions & Clips`, which names the auto-clip feature removed on 2026-10-02.
- It says `com.wordburn.app` "returns 404 on Play". It is live.
- Its Pro paragraph says Pro "adds batch captions, eight more languages", and describes "single clips" as captioning freely with a mark. Both stopped being true in slice 19, when every language became free and the free tier became three videos.
- Its opening still speaks of "five languages".

**`PLAY-CONSOLE.md`** disagrees with itself. §1 creates the app as `Wordburn: Subtitles & Captions`, which is what went live, while §2 pastes `Wordburn: Captions & Clips`. Its §2 description has the slice-19 tier right ("Caption your first three videos free"), but its §8 release notes still describe the old unlimited-single-clip tier.

The fix for all of them is the same. Make `ASO.md` the source again, using the recommendations above: the live title kept, short description A or B, the new opening paragraph, the slice-19 Pro paragraph, and the corrected release notes. Then copy it into `PLAY-CONSOLE.md` §1, §2 and §8. `ASO.md`'s rule against platform names can be relaxed to "not in the title", which is what the policy text supports.

## Conclusion

The keyword plan is mostly about subtraction and timing. In the most valuable field, the live listing already has the right words. What it lacks is claims that are true, and the risk to it is a draft that would swap a keyword for a deleted feature. Wordburn's best openings are exactly its two most literal descriptions of itself: captions that arrive *word by word*, *burned into* the video. The brand name contains half of the second. Head terms like "auto captions" sit in the listing to establish relevance and will rank only once installs, ratings and clean vitals come. Weekly install velocity on long-tail terms, plus reviews that use the words naturally, are the levers that move this app over the next quarter. Re-editing metadata is not.

The localized listings are the larger unpriced opportunity. In Italian, Dutch, Polish and Portuguese, an exact native title beats brands with millions of installs. No competitor can honestly say "in Polish" with the specificity of an app that asks for the language before the video is picked. The limit is quality, not reach: no native speaker has read these strings, the interface they lead to is English, and the downloaded languages have never been timed on a phone. Each localized listing raises expectations the app then has to meet in that language. Write the listings now, but have a native speaker read the 110 characters per locale before they ship.
