## Which of these apps are fully on-device, and what do they give up for it?

### Takeaway
None of the large caption-first apps is on-device: Captions, Zeemo, SubCap, MixCaptions, Submagic, VEED and Zubtitle all upload, and Kaptioned and AutoCap never say. On-device is a crowded small-developer niche that mostly appeared in 2026 — on Android alone, Subly (AndXor), Bytesweavers, Caption.io and Code Stars, none above 5K+ installs — so "on-device, no account" separates Wordburn from the leaders but not from this niche, where Subly already lists 100+ languages, a personal dictionary, a batch queue, 4K export and no watermark, and Bytesweavers lists 50 styles, 50 languages, batch of 10 and speech hints.

### Inferences
- On-device apps trade away reach and certainty, not features: they list 50–100+ languages against 95–125 for cloud apps, depend on a model download and the phone's memory, and none has meaningful distribution (largest caption-first one is 5K+ installs; the on-device clipper is 10K+). No on-device app publishes a speed figure a user could hold it to, and almost none has a review, so nothing is known about how they behave on real phones.
- Subly (AndXor) and Bytesweavers are the direct Android analogues to Wordburn and are the two Dennis should install and test by hand. - **Captune:** "AI Highlight & Enlarge: Use AI to highlight keywords with colors or make important words larger" — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=captions.ai.videoeditor). Mechanism not documented.
- **VEED:** "dynamic subtitles that automatically emphasize keywords as you speak"; "dynamic captions that pop and scale on the keyword as the speaker says it" — [VEED caption generator](https://www.veed.io/tools/auto-subtitle-generator-online/video-caption-generator). Mechanism not documented.

- **ClipCap:** "Random word highlight effects for engagement"; "Random word highlight for emphasis" — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=in.dealempire.clipcap)
- **Clipolette (iOS):** uses audio for clip selection, not word emphasis — "It also hears laughter, applause and cheering, and the moments your voice gets loud." — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/clipolette-ai-clips-captions/id6742556913)

It captions live media on screen; it does not produce a captioned video file.
- Two web searches for caption apps that size or emphasise words from voice volume or loudness (2026-10-02) returned only Expressive Captions and an unrelated web component — [SpeakLouderSphere](https://github.com/Likemiens/SpeakLouderSphere)

### Inferences
- Every vendor that names what its automatic emphasis picks says "keywords" or "important words", which is a text-side judgement (what the word *is*), not a prosodic one (how it was *said*). That is consistent with LLM or NLP selection over the transcript, though none confirms it.
- Wordburn's acoustic pick (loudness against the clip's speech median, held duration, surrounding pause, per CLAUDE.md) therefore has no documented equivalent among caption-first apps. The supportable wording is "no competitor we found says it does this"; "the only app that does this" is not provable from public material, because Captions, Zeemo, Captune and VEED do not disclose their method and could use audio features.
- Google shipping intensity-from-audio in Live Caption shows the idea is established and that users have seen it; it also means the claim is not novel as a concept, only as a feature of a video-caption app.
- ClipCap advertising a *random* highlight as an engagement feature is useful contrast: it shows what "automatic emphasis" can mean in this category when nobody says how the word was chosen.
- Of everything in this matrix, acoustic emphasis is the one Wordburn feature for which no competitor makes an equivalent claim on any platform. It is also the hardest to demonstrate in a store listing, and CLAUDE.md records no test of whether its picks match what a listener hears as stress.

### Gaps
- How Captions' "AI Emphasis", Zeemo's "Auto Highlight", Captune's "AI Highlight & Enlarge" and VEED's dynamic subtitles select words: not disclosed anywhere reached.
- No side-by-side test exists (same clip through each) showing whether their picks track the speaker's stress.

### Inferences
- "Preview equals export" is a claim buyers have reason to care about — the complaints span the largest apps — and almost no vendor makes it. Wordburn's single-layout design (one `layoutCaptionFrame` feeding both renderers, per CLAUDE.md) is the engineering basis for making it, with two honest limits already recorded there: hinted glyph advances differ by under one percent on captions, and by 14 px in a 1080 frame on the watermark's right edge.
- A per-word timing editor with draggable start and end is a leader-tier feature that Captions ships on iOS only and not on Android. On Android, Wordburn's timing sheet (per CLAUDE.md) appears to have no documented equal in this set: Subly, Bytesweavers and Caption.io all describe retiming a cue or segment, not a word. "Appears" because six other apps describe timing too loosely to rule it out.
- Speed is where an on-device app cannot honestly compete on claims: Submagic says about 40 seconds and Kaptioned "seconds", while CLAUDE.md records Wordburn's non-English model at 70–95 s per 20–24 s clip on an emulator and unmeasured on the A54.

### Gaps
- Per-word timing granularity is unconfirmed for Zeemo, AutoCap, Kaptioned, Submagic, SubCap, Bytesweavers and VEED Lite; only installing them would settle it.
- No Reddit thread or YouTube transcript from 2025–2026 comparing preview and export across caption apps was found; evidence is store reviews and blog reviews.
- Google Play shows only a few "most relevant" reviews on the web listing; several quoted above are years old and may not reflect current builds.
