# Competitor marketing claims on batch captioning and auto-clipping, and defensible headlines for Wordburn

Research date: 2026-09-30. Where a quote came from a live fetch of the vendor's own page it is marked **(fetched)**. Where it came from search snippets or a third-party page it is marked **(snippet)** or **(third-party)** and should be re-checked before anyone quotes it publicly. Google Play listing pages (play.google.com/store/apps/details) could not be rendered by the fetch tool, so no Play listing text below counts as verified.

## 1. What do CapCut, Captions, Submagic, Opus Clip, Veed, Vizard, Klap, Descript and Zeemo claim about speed, volume and time saved?

### Takeaway
Across the category the standard speed claim is an **unmeasured multiplier** ("10x faster", "5x faster") or "**minutes / one click**". The volume claim is **clips per long video** ("30+ clips", "10+ clips", "about 5 clips per minute"). None of the pages fetched says what the multiplier is measured against. Nearly every claim is about the web/cloud product: turnaround depends on the vendor's servers, not on the user's phone.

### Inferences
- "10x faster" is category boilerplate (Submagic, Vizard, Opus's older tagline) with no stated baseline. Opus has since walked its own claim down to 5x. A multiplier is the weakest thing Wordburn could borrow, and on-device processing at about 1x realtime cannot support it on wall-clock time anyway.
- The volume numbers (30+, 20+, 10+, "5 per minute") are clip **counts** from long inputs, so each is a promise about the model's output, not about quality. Wordburn's cap of 10 suggested clips per video is lower than every competitor's headline number. Leading with a clip count would lose that comparison. Leading with "no upload, no credits, runs while you do something else" would not.
- User counts, "#1", view-lift percentages and "99%" accuracy are the common garnish. On Google Play, "#1" is explicitly disallowed (see question 3), yet Opus's web headline uses it. The web and the store are held to different rules.

- The Klap "30 seconds" claim came from search snippets only.

## 2. Which offer batch captioning of many separate clips with one preset, and which auto-clip one long video? What are the caps?

### Takeaway
The category is almost entirely **auto-clip one long video**. Batch captioning of **many separate short clips with one style** is rare. It shows up mainly as Submagic's web "batch processing"/API, and as smaller Android apps such as Kalakar and a third-party "SubMagic - AI Captions" Play listing. That makes it a less contested headline than auto-clipping.

### Cited Findings
- **Auto-clip from one long video:** Opus ([opus.pro](https://www.opus.pro/)), Vizard ([vizard.ai](https://vizard.ai/)), Klap ([klap.app](https://klap.app/)), Veed Clips ([veed.io/clips](https://www.veed.io/clips)), Descript Create Clips ([descript.com/clips](https://www.descript.com/clips)) and Submagic Magic Clips ([submagic Magic Clips](https://www.submagic.co/features/magic-clips)) (fetched/snippet)
- **Caps on long inputs:**
  - OpusClip allows up to 10 hours and 30 GB for linked videos, but local uploads are capped at 3 hours (snippet) — [Opus API limitations](https://help.opus.pro/api-reference/limitation)
  - Submagic Magic Clips length limit by plan: Starter 2 min, Professional 5 min, Business 30 min per video; 3,000 MB max file; returns "20+" clips; sold as a +$19/month add-on (snippet) — [Submagic help](https://care.submagic.co/en/article/what-is-the-size-and-video-length-limit-for-each-video-in-submagic-gl84of/), [pricing guide](https://fluxnote.io/guides/submagic-pricing-2026)
  - Veed Clips needs at least 1 minute of speech and at most 3 hours; the upload must be longer than 2 minutes; clips are "mainly a minute or shorter" (snippet) — [Veed help](https://support.veed.io/en/articles/11652474-how-to-use-our-clips-feature)
  - Klap accepts uploads up to 4 hours (snippet) — [Klap tools](https://klap.app/tools)
  - The Descript free plan is "1 media hour / month" and "100 AI credits (one time)" (fetched) — [descript.com/clips](https://www.descript.com/clips)
- **Batch of separate clips:**
  - Submagic advertises processing "multiple videos at once" and "batch-process content without touching the web interface" via API. The Business plan's API allowance is 100 minutes/month (snippet) — [Submagic batch blog](https://www.submagic.co/blog/batch-video-editing), [Submagic API](https://www.submagic.co/api)
  - A Play app titled "SubMagic - AI Captions" (package `com.asil.vas`, which looks like a third party rather than submagic.co) is described as able to "run several videos through the same style at once" (snippet) — [Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.asil.vas&hl=en)
  - Kalakar on Play lists "batch upload and batch render" (snippet) — [Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.kalakar.app)
  - CapCut's "batch edit" means all captions within one timeline (fetched summary) — [CapCut help](https://www.capcut.com/help/batch-edit-to-recognize-subtitle)
  - Captions.ai makes no batch claim on its homepage (fetched) — [captions.ai](https://www.captions.ai/)

### Inferences
- Every auto-clipper is metered, by processing minutes (Opus, Descript), plan tier (Submagic) or credits. "No per-minute credits" is a real differentiator for an on-device app, and it is literally true.
- Wordburn's limits (5–60 min input, up to 10 clips of 20–60 s) are shorter than Opus/Veed/Klap. They beat Submagic's Starter and Professional caps (2 and 5 min).

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether the official Submagic, CapCut or Captions **mobile** apps offer multi-video batch captioning. Their Play listings did not render.
- No competitor caps on the number of videos in a batch were found.

## 3. Google Play policy on metadata and misleading claims, and the character limits

### Takeaway
The title is limited to 30 characters and the short description to 80. Play bans store-performance words ("#1", "Best of Play", "Popular"), price and promotion text ("free for a limited time"), unattributed testimonials, and **any functionality claim the app cannot deliver**. Speed multipliers are not named in the policy, but they fall under "false or misleading information or claims".

### Cited Findings
- "Your app title must be 30 characters or less." (fetched) — [Play Metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en)
- Short description: "80 characters or less"; full description: 4,000 characters (fetched) — [Play store listing best practices](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13393723?hl=en)
- The Metadata policy disallows:
  - emoji and repeated special characters, and ALL CAPS unless part of the brand, in the title, icon and developer name
  - "unattributed or anonymous user testimonials in the app's description"
  - text indicating store performance ("App of the year," "#1," "Best of Play 20XX," "Popular")
  - promotional pricing ("10% off," "free for limited time only")
  - Play programme references ("Editor's choice," "New")

  (fetched) — [Play Metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en)
- The listing must be "a clear and well-written description" that "accurately reflects its functionality" (fetched) — [Play Metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en)
- Misleading Claims: "We don't allow apps that contain false or misleading information or claims, including in the description, title, icon, and screenshots." Examples include misrepresenting functionality and claiming "functionalities that are not possible to implement" (fetched) — [Play Deceptive Behavior policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888077)
- Since 29 Sept 2021, keywords such as "free", "best" and "#1" have been restricted in titles, icons and developer names (third-party summary) — [AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/how-to-prepare-for-new-google-metadata-policy-changes)

### Inferences
- Keep "free", "best", "#1", "fastest" and any user count out of the title and short description. Wordburn's free tier also carries a watermark, which makes "free" doubly risky.
- A wall-clock claim like "10x faster" or "in seconds" could be read as misrepresenting functionality, because a 60 s clip takes about 60 s on-device. A claim about **hands-on time** or **unattended** processing is literally true and checkable.
- "Offline" is close to true but not strictly so, because billing touches the network (per CLAUDE.md). "On-device" / "no upload" is the safer wording for captioning.

### Gaps
- I found no published enforcement data showing which specific speed or number claims got apps suspended. The evidence is the policy text plus forum anecdotes ([Play community thread](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/thread/301427350/misleading-claims-policy-help?hl=en)), which I did not read in full.

## 4. How long does manual captioning take? (grounding for a time-saved claim)

### Takeaway
The most-cited industry figure is **5–10 minutes of work per minute of video** for professional captioners. First-timers take far longer. The honest comparison for a short-form creator today is with **other auto-caption apps, done one clip at a time**, not with hand-typing, and no published figure for that exists.

### Cited Findings
- "it takes about 5-10 times a video's duration to write closed captions"; "A trained transcriptionist will usually take around 4 to 5 hours to transcribe 1 hour of audio or video content"; "a first-timer to closed captioning could take anywhere from 20 to 100 hours to transcribe the same amount of content from scratch" (fetched, 3Play Media, published 28 Oct 2021) — [3Play Media](https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/long-take-manually-caption-videos/)
- Professional captioners "generally average between five and ten minutes of work for every minute of video, with a one-hour video being generally a full day's work" (snippet) — [MSU captioning guide](https://tech.msu.edu/news/2013/05/a-guide-for-captioning-video)
- "It typically takes about 5 minutes to caption 1 minute of video yourself" (snippet) — [Panopto FAQ](https://www.panopto.com/blog/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-about-video-captioning-answered/)
- Submagic's own page quotes a user testimonial of "Save 30 minutes per video" (fetched, testimonial rather than measurement) — [submagic.co](https://www.submagic.co/)

### Inferences
- **Arithmetic example:** 30 clips × 45 s is 22.5 min of video. At 5–10 min per minute by hand that is roughly 1.9–3.75 hours of manual captioning. That is the only published baseline, but it is a strawman against CapCut-style auto-captions.
- A defensible Wordburn claim is structural rather than a multiplier: **N clips need one setup instead of N setups**. Pick files, pick a style, start the queue, then walk away while it runs about in realtime. Hands-on time is roughly flat in N, and wall-clock time is roughly the total audio length. To publish a number, time it: pick 30 clips and start the queue in under X minutes, measured on the A54.

### Gaps
- I found no published measurement of how long a creator spends per clip in CapCut or Captions (import, auto-caption, restyle, export). That is the real baseline and it would have to be measured in-house.
- The 3Play figures date from 2021 and concern professional closed captions, not styled short-form captions.

## 5. Proposed headlines, Play title and short description

### Takeaway
Lead with what is literally true and competitors cannot say: **one setup for many clips, runs unattended in the background, on-device, no upload, no per-minute credits**. Use caps as specifics ("up to 30", "up to 10"), never multipliers.

### Cited Findings (constraints these are built against)
- Title ≤30 characters, short description ≤80, no "#1"/"best"/"free"/emoji — [Play Metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en), [Play listing best practices](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13393723?hl=en)
- No claims of functionality not delivered — [Play Deceptive Behavior](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888077)
- Competitors own "10x faster", "one click" and "30+ clips" — [submagic.co](https://www.submagic.co/), [vizard.ai](https://vizard.ai/), [opus.pro](https://www.opus.pro/)

### Inferences (the proposals)
Headlines (web, ads, screenshots):
1. **"Queue 30 clips. Pick one style. Walk away."** True if the queue caps at 30, applies one style to all, and survives the screen turning off (a foreground service).
2. **"One setup, thirty captioned clips."** A hands-on-time claim with no speed claim.
3. **"Drop in a podcast. Get up to 10 shorts, captioned."** Say "up to"; the 10 is a ceiling, not a promise.
4. **"Captions made on your phone. No upload, no minutes to buy."** The contrast with metered cloud tools (Opus, Descript and Submagic all meter by minutes or plan).
5. **"Your phone captions the queue while you do something else."** The honest "time saved" framing.

Play title (≤30):
- "Wordburn: Captions & Clips" (26)
- "Wordburn: Offline Captions" (26). Only use this if nothing but billing touches the network; "on-device" is safer in body copy.
- "Wordburn - Batch Captions" (25)

Play short description (≤80, counted):
- "Queue 30 clips, pick one style, walk away. Captions made on your phone." (71)
- "Captions for 30 clips at once, or 10 shorts from one long video. Offline." (73)
- "Queue clips, pick a style, walk away. Or cut a podcast into 10 shorts." (70)
- "Caption up to 30 clips in one go, on-device. No upload, no credits." (67)

Avoid, with reasons:
- "10x faster", "in seconds", "instantly": processing runs at about 1x audio length on-device, so these fail the misleading-claims test.
- "#1", "best", "free": explicitly listed in the Metadata policy.
- "Viral": every competitor uses it and it promises an outcome the app does not control.
- "Save X hours": only use it once measured on-device against a named baseline.
- "at once" in the Play text is borderline if the queue runs clips **sequentially**. "In one go" or "one queue" is safer.

### Gaps
- The 30-clip and 10-clip caps, background running and "one style for all" are taken from the brief, not verified in the codebase. CLAUDE.md as of slice 14 describes neither batch nor auto-clip, and says v1 is English-only. Every headline above depends on those features shipping as described.
- "No credits" needs checking against the final monetisation. The current model is a one-time unlock plus a watermark on free exports, which fits.
