# Mobile subscription pricing and monetisation benchmarks for a creator-tool app on Google Play (2025–2026)

Scope note for the writer: the two primary datasets are RevenueCat's *State of Subscription Apps* (SOSA) 2026 edition (115,000+ apps, $16B+, 2025 performance data, published 2026) and Adapty's *State of In-App Subscriptions* 2026 edition (16,000 apps, $3B, 2025 data, published 2026), with their 2025 editions (RevenueCat 2025: 75,000+ apps; Adapty 2025: 11,000+ apps, $1.9B) used for the prior year. The two panels disagree sharply on weekly-plan revenue share, and that disagreement is flagged where it matters. Neither publishes quartile prices by category in the public web edition; only medians are available. "Photo & Video" is a store category on both stores; "Utilities" is the closest other fit for a captioning tool. Wordburn's listed Play prices for reference: Rp 90.000/week, Rp 179.000/month, Rp 690.000/year (≈ USD 5.4 / 10.8 / 41.5 at ~16,600 IDR/USD) — the ratios are weekly×3.85 ≈ yearly, monthly×3.85 ≈ yearly.

## 1. Median (and quartile) prices for weekly, monthly and yearly plans in Photo & Video / Utilities

### Takeaway
Public reports give medians only, not quartiles. RevenueCat (2025 data) puts Photo & Video at $5.99 / $7.99 / $29.99 (weekly / monthly / yearly) and Utilities at $5.99 / $9.99 / $34.99; Adapty's global all-category medians are higher at $7.48 / $12.99 / $38.42, with US and European averages higher still ($8.1 weekly, $15.2 monthly, $44.6 annual in the US per Adapty 2025).

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026 (2025 data), median prices by category: **Photo & Video — weekly $5.99, monthly $7.99, yearly $29.99**; **Utilities — weekly $5.99, monthly $9.99, yearly $34.99**; **Productivity — weekly $5.99, monthly $9.99, yearly $39.99** — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026, Business edition](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-business/)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026 overall medians: weekly $5.99 (unchanged YoY), monthly $8–$10 (flat YoY), annual $34.80 (up from $31.60 YoY). By geography: North America $6.99 / $9.99 / $39.99; Western Europe $6.89 / $9.99 / $39.44; India/SE Asia $4.61 / $3.75 / $18.32 — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2025 (2024 data): "Median prices remained flat year-over-year. However... the most expensive apps got even more expensive, with P75 and P90 prices rising slightly." Also: "Annual subscription prices are generally higher on the App Store than Google Play." Category-level price figures are in the report's "Median price per plan duration by category" chart but are not exposed as text on the public page — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- Adapty SOIS 2026 (2025 data) global medians: **weekly $7.48, monthly $12.99, annual $38.42**; weekly ranges $5.71–$8.94 across regions; European apps charge 29–39% more than North American ones; monthly pricing gap Europe $15.25 vs NA $10.95 — [Adapty State of In-App Subscriptions 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/); [Adapty key findings 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)
- Adapty SOIS 2025 (2024 data) averages: weekly Europe $8.3 (+12.2% YoY), US $8.1 (+12.5% YoY); monthly US $15.2, Europe $13.3; annual US $44.6, Europe $42. Country index vs US: Netherlands +62%, Turkey ≈ 3.5× cheaper — [Adapty SOIS 2025 in 10 minutes](https://adapty.io/blog/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2025-in-10-minutes/)
- Adapty's Utilities category page (2026) reports a 70.5% rise in European annual-plan prices over 2023–2025, "the largest pricing jump of any category-region combination", and that only 1.2% of Utilities apps offer discounts — [Adapty Utilities benchmarks 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/utilities-app-subscription-benchmarks/)
- Adapty's Photo & Video page (2026): high-priced annual Photo & Video apps earn $52 in 12-month LTV vs $19.5 for low-priced ones (2.7× gap); annual-plan LTV for the category $42.4 — [Adapty Photo & Video benchmarks 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/photo-video-app-subscription-benchmarks/)
- Adapty's category pages for both Utilities and Photo & Video print the same $7.48 / $12.99 / $38.42 figures, which are the global all-category medians, so Adapty has not published separate category price medians on the web — [Adapty Utilities benchmarks](https://adapty.io/blog/utilities-app-subscription-benchmarks/); [Adapty Photo & Video benchmarks](https://adapty.io/blog/photo-video-app-subscription-benchmarks/)

### Inferences
- RevenueCat's Photo & Video median ($7.99/mo, $29.99/yr) is the cheapest of the three creator-adjacent categories; Utilities and Productivity sit $2/mo and $5–10/yr higher. A captioning tool positioned as a creator utility can argue for the Utilities/Productivity band rather than the Photo & Video band.
- Adapty's panel prices ~30–60% above RevenueCat's. Adapty's panel is weekly-heavy (see Q5) and skews to aggressive monetisers, so RevenueCat's medians are the safer "market" reference; Adapty's are what the high-pressure end of the market charges.
- Wordburn's USD-equivalent prices (~$5.4 / $10.8 / $41.5) sit at RevenueCat's weekly median, above its Photo & Video monthly median ($7.99) and near its Utilities/Productivity yearly ($34.99–$39.99). The yearly is above the Photo & Video median by ~38%.

### Gaps
- No public source gives P25/P75 prices by category. RevenueCat 2025 says P75 and P90 rose but gives no numbers in the web edition. Quartiles would need the full PDF of either report.
- No source gives Google Play-only price medians by category; RevenueCat only says App Store annual prices are "generally higher".
- A search snippet attributed "$4.99 monthly and $29.99 annually, 44% annual plan selection, 49% trial-to-paid" for Photo & Video to an Adapty Apple Ads page; the page could not be verified and the figures are not repeated in Adapty's SOIS report, so they are excluded.

## 2. Yearly discount against monthly, and the ratio that maximises yearly take-up

### Takeaway
Market medians put yearly at roughly 3–4× monthly (a 65–75% "discount" against 12 months): RevenueCat Photo & Video is 3.75×, Utilities 3.5×, Productivity 4.0×, Adapty global 2.96×. There is no published experiment identifying an optimal ratio; what is published is that the *default-selected* plan, not the discount size, is the lever that moves annual take-up (Q8).

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026 category medians imply yearly/monthly ratios of: Photo & Video $29.99/$7.99 = 3.75×; Utilities $34.99/$9.99 = 3.50×; Productivity $39.99/$9.99 = 4.00×; overall $34.80 vs $8–$10 monthly = 3.5–4.4× — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026 Business](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-business/); [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- Adapty SOIS 2026 global medians imply yearly/monthly = $38.42/$12.99 = 2.96× and yearly/weekly = 5.1× — [Adapty SOIS 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/)
- Adapty SOIS 2025: "Utilities and Photo & Video apps charge significantly more for long-term access, with annual plans costing 6x more than weekly plans"; Education and Health & Fitness charge ~5× weekly for annual — [Adapty SOIS 2025 in 10 minutes](https://adapty.io/blog/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2025-in-10-minutes/)
- A creator-app agency's (Built by Foundry) own recommendation, not third-party data: annual at "5x to 7x the monthly rate", i.e. a 30–45% effective discount, and "default toggle to annual captures 60–70% of paywall selections" from its operational data — [Built by Foundry, annual vs monthly creator apps](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/annual-vs-monthly-subscriptions-creator-apps)
- Adapty SOIS 2026: monthly plans' first-renewal rate drops 8–10 percentage points across price tiers, while weekly stays within a 74–81% band at every price level (i.e. monthly is the most price-sensitive plan length) — [Airbridge summary of Adapty SOIS 2026](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/weekly-vs-annual-subscription-app)
- Adapty SOIS 2026: "9 in 10 subscriptions across all app categories sell at full price" — [Adapty Photo & Video benchmarks 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/photo-video-app-subscription-benchmarks/)
- Adapty SOIS 2026 experiment win rates on LTV: plan-duration tests 58.7%, number-of-plans 57.1%, price-change tests 45.5% (the lowest except visual/copy at 34.6%); price changes also had the lowest conversion uplift (28.3%) — [Adapty key findings 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)

### Inferences
- The market's actual yearly/monthly ratio (3–4×) is well under the 5–7× that agencies recommend; the 5–7× figure is a pricing-page convention, not a measured optimum.
- Wordburn's current ratio (yearly ≈ 3.85× monthly, ≈ 7.7× weekly) is inside the market band on the monthly axis and on the high side of Adapty's 6× on the weekly axis — the weekly is relatively cheap, which makes the "save X%" story weak against weekly and strong against monthly.

### Gaps
- No RevenueCat, Adapty or Superwall publication reports yearly take-up as a function of the discount ratio. The one source that promised a ratio survey (Guy Barner, "Monthly vs. Yearly Subscription: 2025 review", Medium) returned HTTP 403 and could not be read.
- Recurly/Baremetrics figures surfaced in search (median 20% annual discount, 10–30% range) are B2B/web SaaS, not mobile, and were not fetched.

## 3. Trial-to-paid conversion by trial length and platform; share of trials cancelled before charge

### Takeaway
RevenueCat's 2026 edition: ≤4-day trials convert at 25.5% median, 5–9-day at 37.4%, 17–32-day at 42.5%; so a 7-day trial converts ~47% better than a 3-day one and roughly 60–75% of trials are cancelled before charge at any length. On platform, RevenueCat's 2024 edition is the last one to publish a split: App Store 39.0% vs Google Play 28.5%.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026 median trial-to-paid: **≤4 days 25.5%, 5–9 days 37.4%, 17–32 days 42.5%** ("17–32 day trials convert 70% better than short trials") — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2025 (2024 data): 17–32 day trials 45.7% median; 3–7 day trials 26.8%; industry median trial-to-paid 34.8%; median trial start rate 6.2% (P90 20.3%); 82% of trial starts happen the same day as install — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025); [Airbridge paywall decisions (citing RevenueCat 2025)](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/paywall-conversion-structural-decisions)
- Day-0 cancellations as a share of all trial cancellations, RevenueCat SOSA 2026: 3-day trials 55.4%, 7-day 39.8%, 14-day 35.7%, 30-day 31.1%. RevenueCat's trial-length post: 84% of 3-day trial cancellations happen on day 0–1; ~64% of 7-day cancellations on day 0–1; churn drops below 10%/day after day 2 for 14- and 30-day trials — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026 Business](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-business/); [RevenueCat, "The right trial length isn't 7 days"](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/7-day-trial-subscription-app); [9to5Mac on SOSA 2026](https://9to5mac.com/2026/05/27/new-report-shows-annual-app-subscribers-rarely-return-after-they-cancel/)
- Trial-length mix shifted short: in 2024 just over 50% of trials were 5–9 days; in 2025 trials of ≤4 days rose to 46.5% of all trials — [RevenueCat trial-length post](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/7-day-trial-subscription-app)
- Platform split, RevenueCat SOSA 2024 (2023 data): "App Store trials surpass Play Store trials, averaging 39.0% compared to 28.5%". Google Play apps use introductory offers more (66.6% vs 44.1%) — [RevenueCat SOSA 2024](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2024)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2025 (search-result summary, page text not verified): Google Play out-converts the App Store on trials in Business (42.6%) and Media & Entertainment (41.2%) — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- Adapty SOIS 2026 (2025 data): global install-to-trial 10.9%, trial-to-paid 25.6%; Health & Fitness highest at 35.0%, Entertainment lowest 19.1%; 89.4% of trial starts on day 0 — [Adapty SOIS 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/)
- Adapty Utilities (2025 data): 84.7% of apps offer a trial (up from 78.0%); install-to-trial 13.8%; trial-to-paid 26.2%; first-renewal 61.7% — [Adapty Utilities benchmarks 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/utilities-app-subscription-benchmarks/)
- Adapty Photo & Video (2025 data): trial adoption fell from 70.7% (2024) to 62.3% (2025), "the steepest decline of any app category"; weekly-trial funnel: install-to-trial 14.0%, trial-to-paid 30.5%, first renewal 54.9%, refund 7.5%; category trial refund rate 6.4% globally (highest), 14.1% in APAC; 91.2% of trials start on day 0 — [Adapty Photo & Video benchmarks 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/photo-video-app-subscription-benchmarks/)
- Adapty SOIS 2026 paywall placement (16,000 apps): onboarding + trial 1.78% install-to-paid; in-app + trial 0.89%; onboarding without trial 0.82%; in-app without trial 0.76%. Photo & Video onboarding-with-trial 1.35% — [Airbridge (citing Adapty 2026)](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/paywall-conversion-structural-decisions); [Adapty Photo & Video benchmarks](https://adapty.io/blog/photo-video-app-subscription-benchmarks/)
- Superwall 2025 paywall benchmark (search summary, not independently fetched): 3-day trials convert at 3.1% and 7-day at 5.2% of paywall views — [asohack summary](https://asohack.com/blog/7-day-trial-paywall-conversion-data-2025)
- Hard paywall vs freemium, RevenueCat SOSA 2026: D35 download-to-paid 10.7% vs 2.1% (SOSA 2025: 12.1% vs 2.2%, year-one LTV $49.30 vs $24.24, refund rate 5.8% vs 3.4%); "after one year, retention for both models is nearly identical" — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [Airbridge (citing SOSA 2025)](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/paywall-conversion-structural-decisions)

### Inferences
- "Share of trials cancelled before charge" is 1 − trial-to-paid: roughly 75% for ≤4-day trials, 63% for 5–9-day, 57% for 17–32-day (RevenueCat 2026 medians).
- For a tool whose value is proven in one export, a 3-day trial is defensible on cancellation timing (most who leave do so on day 0 regardless) but costs ~12 points of conversion against 7 days.
- Photo & Video is the category *dropping* trials fastest and with the highest refund rates — consistent with a category where the job (one video) is done inside the trial.

### Gaps
- RevenueCat's 2025 and 2026 editions do not publish an overall App Store vs Google Play trial-to-paid median in their web text; the last clean split is the 2024 edition (39.0% vs 28.5%).
- No source breaks trial conversion by trial length *and* platform together.

## 4. Retention and churn by plan length (month 1 / 6 / 12), yearly renewal, and LTV by plan

### Takeaway
RevenueCat SOSA 2025: one-year retention is 3.4% for weekly, 17.0% for monthly and 44.1% for annual plans; ~30% of annual subscriptions are cancelled within the first month, and 95% of lapsed annual subscribers never return. Adapty's 12-month LTV figures favour weekly ($49.27) over annual ($36.51) and monthly ($28–30) for trial-starting cohorts, because weekly's price-per-period is higher than its churn penalty.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat SOSA 2025 (2024 data) year-1 retention by plan: **weekly 3.4% (down from 4.2%), monthly 17.0% (down from 18.8%), annual 44.1% (down from 47.1%)**; "weekly plan retention rarely exceeds 10% at six months"; "Nearly 30% of annual subscriptions are canceled in the first month"; third renewal rates "exceed 77%" — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2024 (2023 data): annual 12-month retention 33.9%, monthly 13.8% — [Built by Foundry citing RevenueCat 2024](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/annual-vs-monthly-subscriptions-creator-apps)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026 (2025 data), as reported by 9to5Mac: annual renewal rate 83.4% overall; monthly ~40%, weekly ~20%; month 1 accounts for 35% of all annual cancellations; first renewal median 23–40%, second 44–64%, third 56–70%; "95% of canceled annual app subscribers never come back", monthly subscribers return at 4× the rate — [9to5Mac on SOSA 2026](https://9to5mac.com/2026/05/27/new-report-shows-annual-app-subscribers-rarely-return-after-they-cancel/)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026 realised LTV: Monthly-1 RLTV median Western Europe $17.89, North America $17.02, IN/SEA $10.59; by monthly price tier: high $35.89, mid $15.78, low $6.67. Utilities D14 RPI $0.23, D60 ≈ $0.34 — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- Adapty SOIS 2026 (2025 data), day-380 retention for trial subscribers: annual 19.9%, monthly 14.2%, weekly 5.5%. 12-month LTV: weekly+trial $49.27, annual+trial $36.51, monthly+trial $28–30. First-renewal: weekly 74–81%, annual 66.3%, monthly 48–58%. Trial subscribers retain 1.4–1.7× better than direct buyers — [Adapty SOIS 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/); [Airbridge summary](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/weekly-vs-annual-subscription-app)
- Adapty SOIS 2026 LTV curves: weekly+trial grows from $7.40 (day 0) to $54.50 (day 380); annual+trial from $42.08 to $49.92 (+18.6%) — [Adapty key findings 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)
- Adapty SOIS 2025 (2024 data): 30-day retention weekly without trial 35%, weekly with trial 42%, monthly 57%; 90-day monthly 43%; one-year weekly 5%, monthly 17%. 12-month LTV: Utilities weekly $58.4, Productivity weekly $53.2, Photo & Video annual $42.4. Refund rates by plan: annual 4.2% (highest), weekly 2.6% (lowest) — [Adapty SOIS 2025 in 10 minutes](https://adapty.io/blog/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2025-in-10-minutes/)
- Adapty Utilities (2025 data): first-renewal retention 58.1% (highest of any category); 12-month LTV all plans $46.30, trial subscribers $68.90 (highest category); one-year retention on annual plans 22.1%; install LTV $1.09 — [Adapty Utilities benchmarks 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/utilities-app-subscription-benchmarks/)
- Adapty Photo & Video (2025 data): install LTV $0.82 (5th of categories; H&F $1.21, Productivity $1.10); hard-paywall LTV $24.0 vs soft $20.0 — [Adapty Photo & Video benchmarks 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/photo-video-app-subscription-benchmarks/)
- Involuntary churn: RevenueCat SOSA 2026 — "Nearly a third of all subscription cancellations on Google Play are involuntary billing failures—more than double the rate on the App Store (14%)"; SOSA 2025 — 28.2% on Google Play vs 15.1% on App Store — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- AI-labelled apps: "41% more revenue per payer, but they churn 30% faster" — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)

### Inferences
- RevenueCat's 83.4% "annual renewal rate" (2026) and 44.1% "year-1 retention" (2025) are different measures: the first is renewals among subscribers reaching the renewal date; the second counts the ~30% who cancel in month 1 and others who lapse before. Don't compare them directly.
- The month-1/6/12 series the brief asked for is only partly published: monthly plans have 30-day 57%, 90-day 43%, 1-year 17% (Adapty 2025); weekly 35–42% at 30 days, <10% at 6 months, 3.4–5.5% at a year; annual 44% at a year (RevenueCat 2025) or ~20–22% (Adapty, trial cohorts and Utilities).
- A weekly subscriber at $5.99 who renews 74–81% per week has an expected lifetime of roughly 4–5 weeks on renewal alone, which lands the realised value near $25–30 before refunds; Adapty's $49–55 day-380 LTV for weekly+trial implies a long tail of survivors doing most of the work.

### Gaps
- No source publishes month-6 retention for annual or monthly plans in a Photo & Video or Utilities cut.
- RevenueCat's 2026 renewal figures above come via 9to5Mac's reading of the report; the report's own page text for those numbers was not reachable in this session.

## 5. Weekly plans: revenue share, which categories use them, and weekly subscriber lifetime

### Takeaway
The two panels disagree: Adapty (2025 data) says weekly plans are 55.5% of all subscription revenue, 73.6% in Utilities and 51% in Photo & Video; RevenueCat (2025 data) says weekly "rarely exceeds 30% of category revenue except Gaming". Both agree weekly subscribers' retention is single digits at a year and that weekly's case is higher LTV per *converting* user, not longer life.

### Cited Findings
- Adapty SOIS 2026 (2025 data): weekly plans 55.5% of all revenue (43.3% in 2023); monthly 11.7% (21.1%); annual 22.5% (29.2%); lifetime/one-time 10.3% (6.4%). **Utilities 73.6% weekly revenue** (49% in 2023; the Utilities page rounds to 74%); **Photo & Video 51% weekly** (49% in 2023), annual only 8% of category revenue; Health & Fitness is the exception at 60.6% annual — [Adapty SOIS 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/); [Adapty Utilities benchmarks](https://adapty.io/blog/utilities-app-subscription-benchmarks/); [Adapty Photo & Video benchmarks](https://adapty.io/blog/photo-video-app-subscription-benchmarks/)
- Adapty SOIS 2025 (2024 data): "Weekly plans grew by 10%, now making up nearly half of all subscriptions" and nearly 50% of in-app revenue — [Adapty SOIS 2025 in 10 minutes](https://adapty.io/blog/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2025-in-10-minutes/)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026 (2025 data): weekly share of *subscriptions sold* is 82% in Gaming (78% in the 2025 edition) and as low as 4% in Health & Fitness; "Weekly plans rarely exceed 30% of category revenue except Gaming"; Gaming's weekly volume "translates to lower revenue share due to price" (Gaming D60 RPI $0.14, monthly RLTV $8.41). Yearly revenue share: Health & Fitness 59%, Shopping 66%, Productivity 77% — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [RevenueCat SOSA 2026 Business](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-business/)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2025: "weekly plans consistently underperform across all price points, with retention hovering around 3–6%" at a year; "Weekly subscriptions may function more as short-term trials rather than long-term revenue drivers" — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- Weekly subscriber lifetime: RevenueCat year-1 retention 3.4% (2025 ed.), ~20% per-period renewal (2026 ed. via 9to5Mac); Adapty first-renewal 74–81% for trial cohorts, 59.2% trial vs 37.0% direct-buy, day-380 retention 5.5% — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/05/27/new-report-shows-annual-app-subscribers-rarely-return-after-they-cancel/); [Adapty key findings 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)
- Adapty SOIS 2026: weekly plans convert 1.7–7.4× better than annual across price tiers; weekly install-to-trial 9.8% vs annual 1.8% vs monthly 0.3% (upper-mid pricing); high-tier weekly plans earn 5.2× more revenue per install than low-tier — [Adapty SOIS 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/); [Adapty key findings 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)
- Adapty Photo & Video: Android weekly LTV is 18% lower than iOS — [Adapty Photo & Video benchmarks](https://adapty.io/blog/photo-video-app-subscription-benchmarks/)
- Apphud case (AEZAKMI Group PDF Scanner, 2023): weekly-with-trial 2.29% conversion vs monthly 1.21% vs weekly-no-trial 1.63%; "weekly subscription with a trial is 2 times better than the original monthly subscription, and also allowed them to double the app revenue" — [Apphud experiments case study](https://apphud.com/blog/experiments-case-study)
- Indie case (Sebastian Szturo via Superwall, 2026): weekly plans carried most of his revenue; 2× revenue January–June 2026 on flat installs after 30+ paywall iterations including weekly-vs-annual and exit-offer tests; no per-plan conversion figures disclosed — [Superwall case study](https://superwall.com/case-studies/how-an-indie-developer-switched-from-revenuecat-back-to-superwall)

### Inferences
- The 55% vs "<30%" disagreement is almost certainly panel composition: Adapty's customer base is weighted to publishers who monetise with short plans and hard onboarding paywalls; RevenueCat's is larger and includes more long-established, annual-led apps. For a new utility on Play, Adapty's panel is probably the closer peer set, RevenueCat's the healthier target.
- Weekly works as a priced trial: it converts best and refunds least (2.6%), but 95%+ of weekly subscribers are gone inside a year. Its revenue case rests on the 20–25% who renew repeatedly, which is why Adapty's weekly LTV only beats annual on trial-starting cohorts.

### Gaps
- Neither panel publishes weekly revenue share for Google Play alone.
- No source gives median weekly subscriber lifetime in weeks; it has to be inferred from per-period renewal.

## 6. Google Play vs App Store: ARPU, conversion, prices, and Play's service fee

### Takeaway
Play earns roughly a third to a half of the App Store per install (RevenueCat 2025: D60 revenue per install $0.14 vs $0.38 globally, $0.35 vs $0.66 in North America) and converts trials ~10 points lower (2024: 28.5% vs 39.0%); 28–33% of Play cancellations are billing failures vs 14–15% on iOS. Play's subscription fee is 15% today and becomes "10% + 5% billing fee" — still 15% for anyone on Play Billing — from 30 June 2026 in the US/UK/EEA, with the rest of the world by 30 September 2027.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat SOSA 2025 (2024 data): "App Store consistently outperforms Google Play... global median D60 revenue [per install] at $0.38 versus $0.14"; North America $0.66 vs $0.35; "Day 35 download-to-paid conversion rates are consistently higher on the App Store than Google Play"; "Annual subscription prices are generally higher on the App Store than Google Play"; billing errors 28.2% of cancellations on Play vs 15.1% on App Store — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026: Play involuntary cancellations "nearly a third" vs 14% App Store; iOS is ~77% of new subscription-app launches (Android ~23%, growing from ~700 to ~3,300 launches/month, Feb 2022–Feb 2026) — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [SOSA 2026 Business](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-business/)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2024: trial-to-paid App Store 39.0% vs Google Play 28.5%; intro offers used by 66.6% of Play apps vs 44.1% App Store — [RevenueCat SOSA 2024](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2024)
- Adapty SOIS 2026: iOS 84.75% of global subscription revenue, Android 15.25%, "5.5x per subscriber" iOS advantage; Photo & Video annual LTV iOS $38.3 vs Android $35.2; Android weekly LTV 18% lower than iOS — [Adapty key findings 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/); [Adapty Photo & Video benchmarks](https://adapty.io/blog/photo-video-app-subscription-benchmarks/)
- Google Play service fees, current (Play Console Help): "15% for the first $1M (USD) revenue earned by the developer each year", "30% for earnings in excess of $1M", and "15% for automatically renewing subscription products purchased by subscribers" regardless of revenue — [Play Console Help: Service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en)
- 2026 changes (Play Console Help): in EEA, UK and US from June 30, 2026; Australia and Japan September 30, 2026. Auto-renewing subscriptions: "10% + 5% billing fee" for new and existing installs; other IAP from new installs "10% + 5% billing fee"; from existing installs "20% + 5% billing fee" or 15% for external web links under the Apps Experience / Games Level Up programs; the billing fee "applies when a user completes a purchase... using Google Play Billing" — [Play Console Help: Service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en)
- Google's March 2026 announcement: subscriptions service fee "10%", 5% billing fee in EEA/UK/US for Play Billing users; rollout EEA/UK/US by June 30, Australia by September 30, Korea and Japan by December 31 [2026], "remaining regions worldwide" by September 30, 2027; developers may use their own billing alongside Play's or link out — [Android Developers Blog, "A new era for choice and openness" (March 2026)](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/03/a-new-era-for-choice-and-openness.html)
- Third-party reading of the same: "the 15% effective rate remains unchanged from January 1, 2022 for most indie subscription apps" — [PricePush, Google Play fees 2026](https://pricepush.app/blog/google-play-subscription-fees-2026-real-math)
- Aggregate claims (secondary, unverified against primary data): "subscription conversion on iOS averages 1.7× Android", "12-month blended ARPU averages 1.6× iOS to Android" — [kirro.io](https://kirro.io/mobile-app-conversion-rate); [Forasoft](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/app-revenue-potential)

### Inferences
- Indonesia is in the "remaining regions" bucket: Wordburn pays 15% on subscriptions now and will still pay 15% (10% + 5% billing) after September 2027 unless it adopts its own billing, which for a no-server app is moot.
- Play's involuntary churn (~30%) is the single biggest platform-specific lever: grace periods and account-hold handling (which the app already models as `suspended`) protect a third of cancellations that iOS apps never see.

### Gaps
- No source gives Google Play-only medians for weekly/monthly/yearly prices, or Play-only trial conversion for Photo & Video or Utilities.
- Google's help page does not say whether the 5% billing fee applies in regions outside EEA/UK/US once the 10% rate reaches them in 2027; PricePush assumes it does.

## 7. Price elasticity: what happened when apps changed prices

### Takeaway
Published mobile experiments mostly show that raising prices did not cut revenue — a higher price more than doubled paid conversion in one Purchasely case, and Adapty finds high-priced annual Photo & Video apps earn 2.7× the LTV of low-priced ones — but price changes are also the experiment type with the lowest win rate (45.5%) in Adapty's data, so the direction is not reliable per app.

### Cited Findings
- Adapty SOIS 2026: price-change tests win on LTV 45.5% of the time (vs 58.7% plan duration, 57.1% plan count, 59.6% trial structure, 62.3% localisation) and have the lowest conversion uplift (28.3%); apps running 50+ experiments show $914,734 median revenue vs $48,848 for apps with one experiment (18.7×); "apps that run experiments earn 40x more" — [Adapty key findings 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/); [Adapty SOIS 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/)
- Adapty SOIS 2026: high-priced annual Photo & Video apps generate $52 in 12-month LTV vs $19.5 for low-priced (2.7×); high-tier weekly plans earn 5.2× revenue per install vs low-tier; monthly first-renewal drops 8–10 points across price tiers while weekly holds at 74–81% — [Adapty Photo & Video benchmarks](https://adapty.io/blog/photo-video-app-subscription-benchmarks/); [Airbridge summary](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/weekly-vs-annual-subscription-app)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026 monthly RLTV by price tier: high $35.89, mid $15.78, low $6.67 — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- Purchasely (parental-control app, Canada, iPhone): "when the price was set slightly higher, the rate of paid subscriptions more than doubled, and the revenue generated almost tripled" — [Purchasely, A/B price test](https://www.purchasely.com/blog/survive-inflation-with-ab-pricetest)
- Apphud case studies (search summary): CoinStats tested $41.99 vs $119.99 over two months and the higher-priced package "attracted more customers than expected"; OctaZone added a 3-month plan priced $10 above the annual and raised LTV 30%; Prosto halved the price but moved to a 6-month plan for +30% revenue — [Apphud experiments case study](https://apphud.com/blog/experiments-case-study)
- Apphud (AEZAKMI, 2023): switching the lead plan from monthly to weekly-with-trial doubled conversion (1.21% → 2.29%) and app revenue; onboarding test lifted conversion 0.8% → 1.29% and ARPU $0.12 → $0.14 — [Apphud experiments case study](https://apphud.com/blog/experiments-case-study)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2025: median prices flat YoY while P75/P90 rose; Adapty 2025: US and European weekly averages up 12.2–12.5% YoY; Adapty 2026: European annual Utilities prices up 70.5% over 2023–2025 — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025); [Adapty SOIS 2025](https://adapty.io/blog/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2025-in-10-minutes/); [Adapty Utilities benchmarks](https://adapty.io/blog/utilities-app-subscription-benchmarks/)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026: "The top 25% of apps grew 80% year-over-year, while the bottom 25% shrank by 33%"; median MRR growth 5.3% — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)

### Inferences
- The published elasticity evidence is survivorship-biased (vendors publish wins). The robust signals are structural: higher-priced tiers retain and earn more per payer in both panels, and the market has been raising prices at the top, not the median.
- For a solo developer, the cheapest "price test" with a positive expected value is plan structure (duration, count, default), not the price itself.

### Gaps
- No RevenueCat or Adapty publication reports an aggregate elasticity (conversion change per % price change). The CoinStats/OctaZone/Prosto figures come from a search summary of Apphud's page and the fetched page only confirmed the AEZAKMI cases; treat the other three as unverified.

## 8. Anchoring and paywall design: number of plans, default plan, "save X%" labels

### Takeaway
Two plans is the most common configuration (41–60% of apps, RevenueCat 2026) and Superwall's data says 2–3 plans lift conversion 44–61% over one; pre-selecting the annual plan roughly inverts the plan mix (37% → 63% yearly) and raised revenue per paywall view from $1.94 to $2.99 in Superwall's cases. No dataset isolates the effect of "save X%" labels.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026, plans per paywall: 1 plan 20–40% of apps, 2 plans 41–60% (most common), 3+ plans 6–27% — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026 Business](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-business/)
- Superwall newsletter (analysis of "popular paywalls in the App Store" and internal case studies; no sample sizes): two products vs one yields a "61%" conversion increase; three products "44% increase in conversions" vs one; setting yearly as default moved yearly selection from "37%" to "63%"; among high-intent users (did not close the paywall) "82% selected the default yearly option"; among low-intent users 54% selected monthly — [Superwall Newsletter Vol. 1](https://superwall.dev/blog/the-superwall-newsletter-volume-1)
- Search summary of the same Superwall material (not separately fetched): annual pre-selected 69% annual selection vs 28% when monthly is default; revenue per paywall view $2.99 (annual default) vs $1.94 (monthly default); a 3-tier paywall with a decoy monthly option $3.14 — [Superwall Newsletter Vol. 1](https://superwall.dev/blog/the-superwall-newsletter-volume-1)
- Adapty SOIS 2026 experiment win rates: number-of-plans tests 57.1% on LTV, plan-duration 58.7%, trial structure 59.6%, localisation 62.3%, price 45.5%, visual/copy 34.6% — [Adapty key findings 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)
- Adapty SOIS 2025: "Three-plan offerings (weekly, monthly, annual) drive stronger LTV across categories"; teams with 50+ paywall experiments "often grow revenue to 100x" — [Adapty SOIS 2025 in 10 minutes](https://adapty.io/blog/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2025-in-10-minutes/)
- Adapty SOIS 2026: hard paywalls generate 21% higher LTV per subscriber than soft; RevenueCat SOSA 2026: hard paywall D35 conversion 10.7% vs freemium 2.1%, with year-one retention "nearly identical" — [Adapty SOIS 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/); [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2025: "90% of users who see the first screen do not convert"; 82% of trials start on install day; Adapty 2026: 89.4% on day 0, 91.2% in Photo & Video — [Airbridge (citing SOSA 2025)](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/paywall-conversion-structural-decisions); [Adapty key findings 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)
- Built by Foundry (agency's own operational data, not a panel): default toggle to annual captures 60–70% of selections; showing a monthly-equivalent price for the annual "improves conversions"; annual-upgrade salvage offer taken by 15% of cancelling trialists; lifetime plan converts 1–3% of paywall views — [Built by Foundry](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/annual-vs-monthly-subscriptions-creator-apps)
- Adapty 2026: trial users' day-380 LTV premium over direct buyers is +85.1% in Utilities, +63.6% Health & Fitness, +50.4% Education, but −13.7% Productivity and −21.2% Lifestyle — [Airbridge (citing Adapty 2026)](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/paywall-conversion-structural-decisions)

### Inferences
- The default-plan effect is the largest documented lever in this whole review (a ~25–40 point swing in annual share) and it is free to implement; the discount size and label are not shown to matter on their own in any dataset found.
- Three plans with the yearly preselected and a monthly-equivalent price is the configuration every vendor's data points at; the risk named by Superwall is that low-intent users default to the cheapest option, which for Wordburn is a weekly priced below the market's own weekly median.

### Gaps
- No published experiment isolates "save X%" labels or the anchor price shown; the evidence is about default selection and plan count.
- Superwall's figures carry no sample sizes or confidence intervals, and the 69%/28% and $2.99/$1.94 pairs were only seen in a search summary of the newsletter, not confirmed in the fetched text (which confirmed 37% → 63% and 82%/54%).
