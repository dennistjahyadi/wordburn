# Competitor subscription pricing for auto-caption / subtitle apps (observed 2026-10-02)

Scope: direct competitors to Wordburn (on-device auto-captions for short vertical video, Android). Every price carries the currency and the surface it was read from. "Observed 2026-10-02" means I fetched the store listing or vendor pricing page myself today; secondary roundups are dated as they date themselves. Nothing is converted.

Wordburn's own Play Console plans, for reference while reading: Rp 90.000/week, Rp 179.000/month, Rp 690.000/year (from CLAUDE.md, A54 run of 2026-10-01).

## Key question 1 — What do the named competitors charge today?

### Takeaway
The category splits into mobile apps priced like RevenueCat's "stabilised" architecture ($4.99–$9.99/month, $30–$80/year, with a $5.99–$7.99 weekly where one exists) and web SaaS priced per seat at $15–$69/month with 33–50% annual discounts. Nobody except the mobile apps sells a weekly, and the two mobile apps with a weekly (Captions, SubCap) price it at 60–100% of their monthly.

### Inferences
- The mobile-native caption apps cluster at $4.99–$9.99/month and $29.99–$79.99/year, which is exactly RevenueCat's 2026 median band (see Q2). Wordburn's Rp 179.000/month is roughly in that band at Indonesian purchasing power; the yearly at Rp 690.000 (0.32 of 12 × monthly) is a steeper discount than anyone in the table except nobody — the steepest observed is 50%.
- Captions is the only true apples-to-apples competitor with a weekly/monthly/yearly triple on a store; its ratios are weekly = 0.60 × monthly and yearly = 0.54 × 12 × monthly.
- CapCut's February 2026 repricing (Pro $179.99/yr, 1080p partly paywalled) is the single biggest recent shift and makes a $30–$80/yr caption-only app look cheap by comparison; the old $89.99 yearly IAP is still in the App Store list, so legacy subscribers may still be on it.
- Web SaaS competitors (Submagic, VEED, Opus, Klap, Descript, Kapwing) price per seat and per minute/credit; none competes on a weekly plan and none is under $12/month even annually, so a $5–$10 mobile monthly undercuts all of them.

### Gaps
- VEED's current monthly/annual prices could not be read from the vendor page (client-rendered); two secondary sets disagree (Lite $19 / Pro $49 monthly vs Basic $18 / Pro $35). Treat VEED as $12–$30/mo annual, $18–$49 monthly, until read in-app.
- Captions' yearly price for Max and Frontier is not published anywhere I found; only Basic's $64.99 is in the App Store IAP list.
- Google Play IAP ranges for Captions Lite and Kaptioned are unverified (identical string and update date on both pages, almost certainly a shared "similar apps" block).
- CapCut's 7-day trial and the "app-store prices a few dollars higher than web" claim come only from secondary roundups, one of which is otherwise stale.
- Zeemo's monthly web price ($9.99) is inferred from the "$8/mo, 20% off yearly" line and a secondary source; the vendor page shows only the yearly-equivalent per-month figure.
- No MixCaptions "Short video subscription" quota or weekly option is documented; the $2.99 monthly's limits are unknown.
- Descript's own page as fetched reversed its monthly/annual labels; I went with the two agreeing secondary sources.

## Key question 2 — Who sells a weekly plan, at what ratio, and what is the typical yearly discount?

### Takeaway
Weeklies exist only in the mobile-native apps — Captions ($5.99, 60% of its $9.99 monthly), SubCap ($7.99, equal to its monthly) and an AutoCap clone (5.99 €/week against 22.99 €/year) — and the category's yearly discount runs from 25% (CapCut) to 50% (AutoCap, SubCap, Opus, Klap), with 33–41% the mode among web tools.

- RevenueCat 2025: "$4.99–$6.99 weekly, $7.99–$9.99 monthly, $29.99–$39.99 annually" has "largely stabilized"; weekly retention "rarely exceeding 10% at six months"; IN/SEA (36%) and MEA (35%) lean on weekly more than other regions — [RevenueCat 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)

### Inferences
- Computed yearly ÷ (12 × monthly): CapCut Pro 0.75; Zubtitle 0.83; Zeemo app 0.67, Zeemo web Pro 0.80; Descript/Kapwing/quso 0.66–0.78; Submagic 0.59–0.63; Captions 0.54; AutoCap, SubCap, Opus Pro, Klap 0.48–0.50. The "safe" middle of the category is 0.55–0.67 (a 33–45% discount); 50% is the floor anyone uses.
- Weekly ÷ monthly: Captions 0.60, SubCap 1.00. Weekly × 52 ÷ yearly: Captions 4.8×, SubCap 8.7×. Both treat the weekly as a high-margin impulse plan, not a cheap entry — consistent with RevenueCat's note that weekly retention is uniformly low.
- Wordburn's Rp 90.000 weekly is 0.50 of its Rp 179.000 monthly (between Captions' 0.60 and the RevenueCat median), and its yearly is 0.32 of 12 × monthly — well under every competitor's 0.48 floor. Either the monthly is high for Indonesia or the yearly is being given away; RevenueCat's "IN/SEA 46–54% lower" applies to all three durations, not only the yearly.

### Gaps
- No category-specific (captions/video-editing) weekly-vs-monthly mix figure exists in public RevenueCat material beyond "33% of Photo & Video apps offer weekly".
- Could not find Google Play (Android) IAP lists broken down by plan; Play shows only a min–max range per app.

## Key question 3 — How do they design the free tier and what does Pro gate?

### Takeaway
Three free-tier designs coexist: watermark + resolution cap (VEED, Zeemo, Opus, Kapwing, Descript, Zubtitle, AutoCap, MixCaptions, Submagic), a count quota (Submagic 3 videos/mo, Zubtitle 2/mo, Zeemo 3/mo, Offline AI Auto Captions 3 projects/day), and a lifetime credit pool with no watermark (Captions). Batch and language are gated almost only by the AI-clipping and enterprise tools, not by the mobile caption apps, which gate the watermark and resolution.

### Cited Findings
- Captions Free: 60–200 lifetime credits, "Basic editing only (captions, …)", "Watermark: None"; paid tiers gate dubbing, AI editing, Clips, AI Twins; Lite gates "captions, dubbing, caption styles, camera, teleprompter" on Android — [Captions Help Center](https://captions.ai/help/docs/subscriptions)
- CapCut: Standard = 1080p export; Pro = "4K/HDR export, full auto-captions, 1TB cloud storage"; "Some 1080p export options previously free are now part of paid plans" — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/app-used-millions-nearly-doubles-subscription-price-overnight-11535999); free tier "export quality capped at 1080p", watermark "sometimes" on templates — [agencyhandy](https://www.agencyhandy.com/informative/capcut-pricing/)
- Zeemo Free: 120 credits/year, 1 min / 200 MB, 720p, watermark; Pro removes watermark, 1080p, 3 min; Expert/Business add 4K, 5-hour videos, "batch generation" / "Video batch upload"; captions in 95 languages on every tier — [zeemo.ai/pricing](https://zeemo.ai/pricing); free = 3 videos/month, 1 min each (secondary) — [search summary of reviews](https://aidemos.com/tools/zeemo)
- AutoCap: free version carries a watermark; subscriptions remove it (secondary roundup) — [search result](https://www.videocaptions.ai/vs/autocap)
- SubCap: without a subscription only manual subtitles; auto captions need the subscription — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/236693/Subcap/)
- MixCaptions: first video free up to 3 minutes; paid "Remove the MixCaptions watermark"; minutes are bought in packs — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mixcaptions-video-captions/id952426779)
- Submagic: Free 3 videos/month with watermark, 1:30 cap; Starter removes watermark, 15 videos, 2-min cap; Pro 40 videos, 5-min; Business 100 videos, 30-min, 4K; "Magic Clips" (AI clipping) and "AI Translate captions" on every paid tier; batch only via API credit packs — [submagic.co/pricing](https://www.submagic.co/pricing), [SaaSworthy](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/submagic.co/pricing)
- VEED Free: watermark on every video, 720p, 10-minute cap, limited subtitle minutes; translation/dubbing in 50+ languages only on Pro — [fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/veed-io-pricing-2026)
- Opus Clip Free: 60 credits, 1080p, "Has watermark", "No editing", 3-day expiry, 9:16 only; Starter removes watermark, adds "AI animated captions in 20+ languages" (multi-language transcription is Starter+); Pro adds all aspect ratios, scheduler, dubbing, 2 seats — [opus.pro/pricing](https://www.opus.pro/pricing)
- Klap: no free tier, one free video; captions 52 languages on all plans; dubbing only Pro+ — [scalereach](https://www.scalereach.ai/blog/klap-review-for-short-form-teams)
- quso.ai Free: 75 credits/month, 720p, 7-day retention; Lite unlocks unlimited 1080p — [quso.ai/pricing](https://quso.ai/pricing)
- Descript Free: 60 media minutes/month, 720p with watermark; Hobbyist 10 hrs 1080p watermark-free; "Batch file export" Creator+; dubbing Business only; transcription 25 languages, caption translation 61 — [descript.com/pricing](https://www.descript.com/pricing)
- Kapwing Free: 10 credits, watermark, 720p, 4-minute exports, 10 subtitle minutes, 5 translation minutes; Pro 1,000 subtitle minutes, 500 translation minutes — [kapwing.com/pricing](https://www.kapwing.com/pricing)
- Zubtitle Free: 2 videos/month, watermark, 720p; paid removes watermark, 4K — [zubtitle.com/pricing](https://zubtitle.com/pricing)
- Offline AI Auto Captions (on-device): "The free version includes up to three captioning projects per day"; "Pro unlocks unlimited daily projects, background-safe batch processing for up to 10 videos, larger high-accuracy models, and advanced MP4 rendering and styled ASS export"; 50 languages on every tier; "Contains ads" — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=tech.bytesweavers.offlineautocaptions)

### Inferences
- The watermark is the near-universal free-tier marker; only Captions (credits, no watermark) and Klap (no free tier) deviate. Wordburn's watermark-on-free matches the category default.
- Nobody among the mobile caption apps gates languages: Captions advertises 100+, Zeemo 95, Opus 20+, Klap 52, the on-device app 50, all on the lowest paid tier or free. Wordburn gating eight of nine languages behind Pro is unusual; the defensible version of it is the model download (874 MB) rather than the language.
- Batch is gated only by the tools whose buyers are agencies (Zeemo Business, Submagic API, Descript Creator+) and by the one on-device competitor (10 videos, Pro). Wordburn's batch-of-20 behind Pro is in line with that.
- Minute quotas are a cloud-cost artefact (Zeemo credits, Kapwing minutes, Descript media minutes, Opus credits); an on-device app has no reason to copy them, and "unlimited minutes" is a differentiator none of the cloud tools can match at $5–$10.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm from a primary source whether AutoCap (ZipoApps) or Captions Lite's free export carries a watermark on Android today.
- Zeemo's Expert monthly price and whether its app Pro ($9.99) maps to web Pro or Expert is not documented.

## Key question 5 — Are there Android-only or on-device competitors, and how do they price?

### Takeaway
There is exactly one shipping on-device Android competitor with a Play listing — "Offline AI Auto Captions" (Bytesweavers), 500+ installs, ad-supported, $3.99–$19.99 IAP range, free at 3 projects/day — plus open-source whisper.cpp/Vosk projects on GitHub and F-Droid that are free and unmonetised; on-device is not a price tier anyone is competing on.

## Key question 6 — Recent price changes (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
Three documented moves: CapCut roughly doubled Pro's yearly to $179.99 and introduced a $9.99 Standard tier (February 2026, with 1080p partly paywalled); Descript switched to media-minute plus AI-credit billing (September 2025); Captions renamed Pro→Basic and Scale→Frontier and moved its product onto credits, which is the main source of complaint.

### Cited Findings
- CapCut: Pro yearly "~$77" → $179.99, monthly $19.99, Standard/Pro/Team structure, reported 2026-02-17; renewals at the new rate from "as early as February 20, 2026"; no formal announcement — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/app-used-millions-nearly-doubles-subscription-price-overnight-11535999)
- Descript: "post-September 2025 overhaul when Descript transitioned from transcription-hour to media-minute billing with metered AI credits" — [Sonix](https://sonix.ai/resources/descript-pricing/)
- Captions: "Basic Plan (formerly Pro)", "Frontier Plan (formerly Scale)" — [Captions Help Center](https://captions.ai/help/docs/subscriptions); "The app now sells credits, seats and AI usage tiers instead of one flat editing subscription" — [forkoff](https://forkoff.xyz/compare/captions-ai-pricing)
- vidyo.ai → quso.ai rebrand, January 2025, "same plan" — [quso.ai](https://quso.ai/vidyo-ai)
- Submagic: no price change found; the recurring complaint is per-member seat multiplication — [soku.ai](https://soku.ai/alternatives/submagic/review)

### Inferences
- The direction of travel in 2025–26 is up and towards metering (credits, media minutes, seats). A flat on-device subscription with no minute cap is positioned against that trend, and the CapCut backlash quotes ("not worth it compared to alternatives at $50 annually") name the price point a caption-only yearly should sit under.
