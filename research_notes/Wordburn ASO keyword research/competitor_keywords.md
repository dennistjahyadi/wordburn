# Competitor Play Store keywords: what dedicated caption apps target, and what nobody owns

All listings read on **2026-10-02** from Google Play's own data (the listing pages and Play web search, `hl=en&gl=US`), pulled with the `google-play-scraper` library, which reads the same page a browser gets. Install bands are Play's own "N+" band; where Play hides the rating (too few reviews) it is marked "–". Search ranks are Play **web** search for US English. The in-app search on a phone is personalised and may order things differently. 39 caption or subtitle apps were read in full; general editors (CapCut, InShot, VN, Edits, VEED's web wrapper) were excluded under the caption-apps-only rule.

Method note for the writer: "Captions AI…" in a title almost never means the Mirage-owned Captions app. On Android, Mirage publishes only **Captions Lite** (developer "Captions", `com.nocap.captions`). Every other "Captions AI" title belongs to an unaffiliated developer — [Play developer page: Captions](https://play.google.com/store/apps/developer?id=Captions&hl=en&gl=US). The official Submagic has no Play app: the "SubMagic - AI Captions" listing is by Asilşah Medya, and the other two "Submagic" results are "app guide" listings — [Play search: submagic](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=submagic&c=apps&hl=en&gl=US).

**Wordburn's live listing does not match `ASO.md`.** On 2026-10-02 `com.wordburn.app` is live as **"Wordburn: Subtitles & Captions"** (30 characters), developer Venturefold Labs, 1+ installs. Its short description is "Auto captions & subtitles for your video. Offline, on-device, pay once." Its full description still sells "NINE CAPTION STYLES", offline use and pay once — [Play: Wordburn](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.wordburn.app&hl=en&gl=US). It already ranks **#15 for "word by word captions"** — [Play search](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=word+by+word+captions&c=apps&hl=en&gl=US). `ASO.md` drafts "Wordburn: Captions & Clips", which still names auto clip; that feature was removed on 2026-10-02.

## Q1. Exact titles, short descriptions, developers, installs and ratings, and who shows up for the head terms

### Takeaway
About 14 caption-first apps with 100K+ installs fill page one for every head term. Six are 1M+ (Zeemo 5M+, Captions Lite, Captions AI by Pixster, AutoCap, Dubs, Captiono). Nearly every title is built from the same four words: **Captions, Subtitles, AI, Auto**. Below them sits a long tail of 2025–2026 entrants with fewer than 10K installs, several of them on-device or offline: Subly, Offline AI Auto Captions, SubLocal, Identiq's Auto Captions and Parismita.

### Inferences
- The head of the category is crowded with **look-alike "Captions AI" titles** that ride the Captions/Mirage brand: Pixster, Captune, Inverse AI, Coloring Laze, Super Jiu, and AI Kids, whose listing opens "No need for an iPhone. Introducing Captions AI". "Captions" as a bare word therefore means an exact-match fight against a dozen apps. "Subtitles" alone is lost to movie-subtitle downloaders.
- AutoCap has not been updated since 2025-09-18 and Zeemo has pivoted to AI generation, so two of the 1M+ incumbents are drifting off pure captioning. That may soften competition for "auto captions" over time; this is an inference, not measured.
- The fresh entrants of August–October 2026 converge on a new set of claims — on-device, word-level timing, karaoke, SRT, no watermark — which is Wordburn's own territory.

### Gaps
- Keyword search volume and difficulty. Play publishes neither, and AppTweak, AppFollow and 42matters gate them behind accounts, so the frequency counts here are supply-side: what listings say, not what users type.
- Install bands for the iOS-only competitors (SubCap on iOS, Submagic) are outside Play's scope.
- Rankings in the in-app Play search and in other countries, Indonesia above all, were not sampled.

## Q2. Which words appear in titles, and how often

### Takeaway
Across the 39 listings, **"Captions"/"Caption" is in 35 titles, "AI" in 23, "Subtitle(s)" in 17, "Auto" in 13 and "Video(s)" in 12**. Only five titles say Maker, Generator or Creator, one says Editor, three say Teleprompter, and none says Translate, Karaoke, Word, Style or Batch. The dominant title grammar is `<Brand>: AI/Auto Captions & Subtitles`.

- Title length: most established titles use 25–30 of the 30 characters. Zeemo, Pixster, AutoCap and MixCaptions use 29, and Super Jiu uses 30. Captions Lite (13) and AI Captions (11) are short because they rank on their brand. Same source.
- Play's title limit is 30 characters — [Google Play Metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en).

### Inferences
- "Captions" plus "Subtitles" in the title is table stakes. Wordburn's live title already has both. Dropping "Subtitles" for "Clips", as `ASO.md` drafts, gives up the second-most-common title word for a feature that has since been removed.
- "AI" is in 23 of 39 titles, which makes it nearly meaningless as a differentiator but probably still a matched search token. Wordburn's live title omits it, which is a deliberate trade, not an oversight. Whether "AI" earns its characters is an A/B question for a store listing experiment.

### Gaps
- No public data on whether Play's ranking weights "AI" as a term, or treats "Captions & Subtitles" and "Subtitles & Captions" differently.

## Q3. Do listings name TikTok, Reels, Shorts or YouTube, and does Play allow it?

### Takeaway
Platform names are **almost absent from titles** (one adjacent app, Mojo's "Reels") but **common in short descriptions (7 of 39) and near-universal in full descriptions**: 27 of 39 name TikTok, 29 name Reels or Instagram, and 29 name Shorts or YouTube. Play's written policy bans implying an affiliation, not mentioning a platform. The category's practice is to keep platform names out of the title and put them in the short and full descriptions as "for TikTok, Reels & Shorts".

- **Google's metadata policy** for title, icon and developer name: "Don't use emojis, emoticons, or repeated special characters… Avoid ALL CAPS unless it is part of your brand name", no text indicating "store performance or ranking, price or promotional information", and "Avoid using repetitive or unrelated keywords or references" — [Google Play Metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en). No line in it forbids naming another platform.
- **Google's impersonation policy** prohibits "app icons, descriptions, titles, or in-app elements that could mislead users about your app's relationship to someone else or another app", for example "Titles like 'Justin Bieber Official' are not allowed without the necessary permissions" — [Google Play Impersonation policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888374?hl=en).
- The metadata policy also bans "unattributed or anonymous user testimonials in the app's description" — [Google Play Metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en). Kaptioned's "Trusted by 800,000+ creators" and SUBCAP's "35% MORE ENGAGEMENT" are stats rather than testimonials.

### Inferences
- Naming platforms descriptively ("captions for your TikTok, Reels and Shorts videos") in the short or full description is common practice and does not on its face break the written policy, since it implies no affiliation. `ASO.md`'s blanket rule ("Play's metadata policy discourages other brands' names") is stricter than the policy text. The risk sits in the title and icon, where an implied affiliation is likeliest to be read. A reviewer's call can still go either way, and trademark owners can file complaints independently of Play policy.
- Seven of 39 short descriptions spend characters on platform names, and almost every full description does. For a phrase like "captions for reels", Wordburn would be competing with the full-description mentions of nearly every rival.

### Gaps
- No primary source found on whether Play's ranking indexes the full description for "tiktok"/"reels" queries, beyond the general understanding that the full description is indexed on Play (unlike the App Store). TikTok's and Meta's own trademark-use guidelines for app listings were not checked.

## Q5. Gaps: terms for what Wordburn really does that few competitors target

### Takeaway
Five terms that Wordburn can truthfully claim are owned by **no established app in its title or short description**:

1. **Batch captions** — no Play result for "batch captions" is about batching, and only three tiny apps mention it.
2. **Word by word / word highlight** — one 1K+ app has it in the title.
3. **Karaoke captions** — CapFlow alone, and the search otherwise returns singing apps.
4. **Personal dictionary** — Subly alone.
5. **SRT export for creator captions** — SRT queries are dominated by file utilities rather than creator caption apps.

"Edit every word" and "N caption styles" are contested only in full descriptions. Translation, emoji and "100+ languages" are crowded, and Wordburn should not claim the first two at all.

### Cited Findings
- "batch captions" on Play returns Captions Lite, Pixster, InClips, AI Kids, AI Captions and Instagram caption/quote apps. None is about multi-video processing — [Play search](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=batch+captions&c=apps&hl=en&gl=US). Batch is mentioned in descriptions only by Bytesweavers (10 videos, Pro), Subly and the Asilşah "SubMagic", none of them above 1K installs — see the Q4 sources.
- "word by word captions": the only app with the phrase in its title or short description is Word Captions (1K+, "No Watermark, Unlimited Length, Word by Word AI Subtitles on Videos") — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.cooldude.wordcaptions&hl=en&gl=US). Wordburn already ranks #15 for it — [Play search](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=word+by+word+captions&c=apps&hl=en&gl=US).
- "karaoke": only CapFlow (10K+) puts it in a short description ("auto captions, karaoke, SRT export, no watermark") and it ranks #1 for "karaoke captions". Positions 2 and 4–20 are singing apps — [Play search](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=karaoke+captions&c=apps&hl=en&gl=US).
- SRT queries ("srt subtitle generator", "subtitle editor") return SRT editors, converters and sync tools almost exclusively — [Play search](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=srt+subtitle+generator&c=apps&hl=en&gl=US). Only 4 of 39 caption apps put SRT in the short description. Source: computed from Q1.
- Dictionary: Subly alone among competitors — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=biz.andxor.subly&hl=en&gl=US).
- "One style for every video" or saved looks: only the Asilşah "SubMagic" says "set it up once, use it on every video after with one tap" — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.asil.vas&hl=en&gl=US).
- Wordburn's real feature set — nine spoken languages, 18 styles, per-word editing (nudge, split, merge, drag), personal dictionary, burn-in plus .srt, batch of up to 20 on Pro, free tier of three watermarked videos, no translation, no auto-emoji, no B-roll, Android only — comes from the brief and the project's `CLAUDE.md`, not from a web source.

### Inferences
- **Highest-value gap terms for Wordburn**, in rough order of truthfulness times openness:
  1. "batch captions", "caption multiple videos" or "bulk subtitles" — open, and true for Pro, though batch has only run on an emulator.
  2. "word by word captions" or "word highlight" — thinly owned, and Wordburn already ranks.
  3. "karaoke captions" — one owner.
  4. "edit every word" — present only in full descriptions.
  5. "SRT" — move it into the short description to meet the file-tool searchers.
  6. "subtitle maker" or "caption maker" — in five titles, but no 1M+ app owns them in the title.
- **Terms to avoid**: "translate" (false for Wordburn), "emoji" (false), "100+ languages" (false), "no watermark" (false on the free tier), "AI video editor" (crowded and off-positioning), and bare "subtitles" (lost to movie-subtitle downloaders).
- Nine named languages may be a long-tail asset rather than a weakness. A short description or full description naming "Spanish, German, French, Indonesian…" can match "subtitles Indonesian" or "subtitulos" style queries in localized listings, but no localized search was sampled here.
- "Offline" and "on-device" are now contested by four or five small apps. Wordburn's live listing still leads on them, while `ASO.md` drops them; the live listing and the draft disagree and need reconciling.

### Gaps
- Search volume for each gap term is unknown, because no public Play keyword-volume source is available without an account. A term nobody targets may also be a term nobody types; "batch captions" in particular may have negligible volume.
- Localized listings (es, de, fr, id, pt, it, nl, pl) and their competitors were not examined.
